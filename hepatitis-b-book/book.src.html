Before anything else

5 things you have probably been told that are not true

Almost everybody who gets this result in Nigeria hears these five things along with it, usually from somebody who meant well. All five are wrong, and believing any one of them can cost you.

Myth 1

“If I feel fine, it cannot be serious.”

The truth

Feeling fine is the symptom.

Your liver has no pain nerves inside it. It can lose a great deal of its working tissue and say absolutely nothing. By the time somebody’s eyes turn yellow, years have already gone. Almost everybody who ends up in real trouble with this virus felt perfectly well for most of the journey there.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 1.

Myth 2

“Somebody’s herbs can flush it out.”

The truth

Nothing flushes it out, and some of those bottles do damage of their own.

No herb, no tea, no root has ever been shown to clear hepatitis B from a human body. Think about it small: if one of them worked, would it still be in an unlabelled bottle on a table by the roadside? Some of them also do harm. Herbal mixtures are a known cause of liver injury, and the organ that has to process them is the same one already carrying the virus.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

Myth 3

“This means I am going to die soon.”

The truth

Most people living with hepatitis B die of something else entirely, decades later.

What decides how this goes for you is whether somebody is keeping an eye on it. Checked properly, and treated in the years when treatment is needed, most people carry this for a full lifetime and it is never the thing that ends it.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 2.

Myth 4

“People can catch it from my food, my cup, my greeting.”

The truth

They can’t. It doesn’t pass through food, cups, hugging or mosquito bites.

It travels through blood, and from mother to baby at birth. Sharing a plate of rice won’t pass it on. Most of the wickedness people with this virus suffer in this country comes from neighbours who believe otherwise. You don’t need separate plates, you don’t need to eat alone, and you can keep cooking for your family.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 9.

Myth 5

“There is nothing anybody can do anyway.”

The truth

There is a great deal, and most of it is cheap.

Three injections protect everyone living in your house, for life. Taking alcohol out does more to slow the damage than any food will. A scan every six months finds trouble while it is still small and can be treated. You can do all of this yourself, and none of it costs much.

Dealt with properly in Chapters 4 and 9.

If you believed even one of them, don’t worry. You haven’t lost anything yet. Keep reading.

About this book

You are reading this because of a piece of paper.

Maybe it came from a test before a wedding. Maybe you went to donate blood and somebody called you aside. Maybe you were pregnant, and the nurse's face changed while she was reading.

However it reached you, one thing is true this morning: you feel completely fine, and you have been told that you are not.

That’s the strange part. You have no pain and no fever, just one word on a result sheet, reactive, and after that nobody explained anything. If nobody sat you down and talked you through it, you are not alone. That’s how it goes in most places: they give you the result and call the next person.

So let me explain it to you now.

iii

What nobody said to you that day

You are not going to die from this any time soon, and quite possibly not from this at all.

About one in every twelve Nigerian adults is carrying the same virus you are, and most of them don’t know it. Many will only find out ten or fifteen years from now, when something finally starts to hurt, and by then they will have fewer choices than you have today.

It may feel like the worst day of your life, but finding out early is the biggest advantage anybody with this virus can have. This book is about making good use of it.

Inside this book

  • The tests that show what is really going on in your liver, and the ones your first hospital visit probably skipped
  • Why you feel perfectly healthy, and why that makes this virus so dangerous
  • The everyday Nigerian foods that can hurt your liver while you are trying to protect it
  • The mixtures sold to "flush" hepatitis out of your body, and why you should leave them alone
  • How to protect your husband or wife, your children, and your brothers and sisters, starting this week
  • What to say, who to say it to, and what you never have to explain to anybody

What this book will not do

It will not tell you that hepatitis B can be washed out of your body by food, by herbs, by hot water, by bitter roots, or by prayer alone. It cannot. Anybody charging you money for that is charging you for a story.

Some of them will do worse than take your money. A number of the mixtures sold for "liver cleansing" are hard on the liver itself, and your liver is already under strain. We come back to this in Chapter 3, because it matters more than almost anything else in these pages.

This is what can be done. This virus can be watched properly. It can be held down with medicine when medicine is needed. Damage that has already started can often ease once the virus is under control. And the people sleeping in your house tonight can be protected completely, this month, for less than you think.

Chapter one

01

What just happened

Reactive. Positive. HBsAg +ve. Three different ways of writing the same sentence: there is a virus living in your liver, and it has been there a long while.

Probably much longer than you think. Most Nigerians carrying hepatitis B didn’t catch it last year from anything they did. They got it as a baby or a small child, at birth, or from a cut, a shared blade, or an injection given in good faith, and it has been sitting there ever since, mostly doing very little.

So please hear this: you did nothing wrong. Put that guilt down right there where you are standing. You will need your strength for other things.

Your liver, in one paragraph

Everything you swallow goes to your liver first. Food, drink, medicine, whatever the chemist sold you last week, all of it passes through the liver before it reaches the rest of your body. Your liver decides what gets used and what gets thrown out. It stores your energy. It makes the substance that stops a cut from bleeding forever. It is the busiest organ you have.

It is also the only organ that can rebuild itself after damage. Remember that, because a lot of this book depends on it.

Why you feel fine, and why that is the problem

Your liver can’t feel pain. The pain nerves are only in the thin covering around it, and that covering doesn’t complain until things have gone very far.

So a liver can lose a great deal of its working tissue without giving you any warning, or anything you would think to take to a doctor. By the time the yellow shows in somebody’s eyes, years have already passed, and the conversation in the consulting room is a very different one.

This is why hepatitis B is called a silent disease. You are feeling fine, fine well well, and that, my friend, is the trouble.

Only the tests can tell you how your liver is doing, however well you feel.

The long road, and where you are standing

Damage to a liver does not happen in one night. It happens in stages, over years, and each stage is quieter than it deserves to be.

Figure 1: the road, and where people usually notice

Look at that picture again. The whole left side of it can last for years, and you feel nothing at all. I’m not showing you this to frighten you. It is why this book is built around ninety days of action instead of waiting for symptoms, because nothing is going to tap you on the shoulder.

Two words you will hear

Acute means the virus arrived recently. Most healthy adults who catch it as adults fight it off within six months and are finished with it for life.

Chronic means it has stayed longer than six months. If you caught it as a baby, it is almost certainly chronic, because a young immune system doesn’t know how to fight it off. This is the situation most Nigerian adults are in, and it is the situation this book is written for.

Three things about that table

You do not need all four to need treatment. Scarring on its own is enough, whatever your viral load and ALT are doing. That is the biggest change the WHO made in 2024, and it is the reason someone with a normal ALT can still be told to start.

Those ALT lines are lower than the ones printed on most lab slips. A slip saying "normal up to 40" is using a general reference range, not a hepatitis B one. American guidelines use 35 and 25. If yours falls between the two, talk it through with your doctor before you accept that it is normal.

One high reading is not a diagnosis. ALT is judged on two readings over six to twelve months, because it moves with an ordinary illness, a hard week, or a bad night.

If you are pregnant

There is one number that matters more than any other, and it has a deadline. A viral load of 200,000 IU/mL or above in the last three months of pregnancy means a tablet is recommended to protect the baby. Ask for that test by name, and ask early enough that there is time to act on it.

Write this down before your next appointment

"Doctor, what is my ALT, and what is my viral load?"

"Has anybody checked whether I already have scarring?"

Ask both out loud, write the answers in the log at the back of this book, and you will already be ahead of most people in that waiting room.

Chapter two

02

The two questions that decide everything

Two people can walk into the same clinic on the same morning with the same result, and walk out into two completely different lives.

One is told to come back in six months. The other is started on a tablet she will take every day for many years. Neither of them is being treated badly. They are just at different stages, and two questions decide which stage you are at.

Question one: is the virus active?

A quiet virus and a busy virus need different things. Three results answer this question together: your ALT, your viral load and your HBeAg. None of them decides anything on its own, so if only one was ordered, ask about the other two.

Question two: has it already caused scarring?

Almost nobody asks this one, and it is the more important of the two. Scarring, which doctors call fibrosis, is the liver slowly replacing working tissue with something tough, a bit like the hard skin that forms on a farmer’s palm.

It can be measured two ways. A special scan presses gently on the liver and measures how stiff it has become. Stiff means scarred. Or a score can be worked out from blood tests you may already have had, which costs nothing extra and is a reasonable place to start if money is tight.

The four rooms

Put those answers together and you land in one of four situations. Doctors have long names for them. This is what they mean in plain English.

Figure 2: the four situations

The room nobody warns you about

Look at the bottom right. A virus that has been settled for fifteen years can become active again without any symptom to tell you. That’s the best reason to keep your six-month appointments, even in the years when you feel wonderful and nothing has ever gone wrong.

8

If you are told you need medicine

It is usually one tablet, once a day. The two most commonly used are tenofovir and entecavir. Both are very good at what they do.

This is what they do. They hold the virus down, often so far down it can’t be found in your blood at all, and that stops the ongoing injury. Once the injury stops, the liver gets the chance to repair itself, which no other organ can do. Scarring that has already formed often eases once the virus has been kept down for long enough. It doesn’t happen for everyone, and it takes time, but it happens often.

What they can’t do is remove the virus from your body. It stays in the background, and that’s why the tablet is usually long-term, often for life.

Never stop the tablet on your own

Of all the safety advice in this book, this is the most important. Stopping hepatitis B medicine suddenly can let the virus come roaring back, and that rebound can do serious harm quickly, sometimes more than the virus did before you started.

People stop for ordinary reasons: the money ran out, they felt fine, or somebody at church told them they had been healed. If any of that is about to happen to you, tell your doctor first and let them plan it with you. There are cheaper options, and there are safe ways to stop, but you have to go back and ask.

The same goes for this book. It is here to help you learn. It can’t see your results and it doesn’t replace the doctor who can, so nothing in it is ever a reason to delay, change or stop a medicine you have been given.

If you are told you do not need medicine yet

This is good news, but it doesn’t mean you are finished with the hospital. "Watch" means blood tests once or twice a year and a scan every six months, for life. The value of being watched is that the moment anything changes, somebody catches it.

Being watched means somebody is checking, on a schedule, for the changes you can’t feel.

Chapter three

03

The silent enemies

Your liver is already carrying the virus. Everything in this chapter adds extra weight on top of that, and the good news is that you can put every one of these down yourself.

Read this chapter twice. If you only act on one chapter in this whole book, it should be this one, because avoiding harm is faster and cheaper than repairing it.

1. Alcohol

I won’t waste your time with this one. The safe amount is none.

That means none at weekends and none in the form of “only beer”. Alcohol and hepatitis B together do far more harm than either one alone, and a liver dealing with both scars much faster.

Before you start making an exception in your mind: beer is alcohol, palm wine is alcohol, and those bitters they advertise as good for men, sold in small bottles at the junction, are alcohol too. I know somebody is reading this and already deciding which one doesn’t count. Drop it. If you drink, stopping is the most useful change you can make, and it will do more for you than every food in Chapter 5 put together.

14

2. The mixtures sold to cure you

Somebody has probably offered you one already, maybe even this morning.

Shine your eyes here. Of everything in this book, this is the page most likely to save your life.

It might be agbo, a bottle from a neighbour you trust, a powder from a man on the radio, or a tea in a tin with a testimony attached. They all promise the same thing in the same words: that it will flush it out, wash the liver or clear it completely.

Two things are true about these, and the second one is worse than the first.

The first is that they do not work. No herb, no tea, no root, no mixture has ever been shown to clear hepatitis B from a human body. Think about it small: if one of them worked, would it still be in an unlabelled bottle on a table by the roadside? It would be in every hospital in the world before the year finished.

The second is that some of them cause real harm. Herbal preparations are a recognised cause of liver injury, and in this part of the world they are a documented reason people arrive in hospital with livers failing. Herbs themselves aren’t evil. The trouble is that nobody can tell you what is in an unlabelled bottle, how much of it, or whether this week’s batch is stronger than last week’s.

And somebody will tell you their uncle drank something and it cleared completely. Ask that person one simple question: “Please, can I see the test result before, and the test result after?” Then wait. You will be waiting for a long time.

Your liver has to process everything in that bottle, and it is already carrying a virus. Asking it to do both is like experimenting on a part of your body you can’t replace.

If you have already been taking one

You haven’t ruined anything. Stop taking it, tell your doctor what you were taking (bring the bottle with you if you still have it), and ask for a liver function test. That’s all you need to do, and nobody is going to shout at you.

15

3. Paracetamol, and the trap inside it

Paracetamol is in almost every house in Nigeria, and taken properly it is one of the safer painkillers for a liver.

The trap is that it hides in other medicines. It is inside many cold-and-catarrh sachets, many flu tablets, many "extra strength" pain mixtures, often under a different name on the packet. People take a paracetamol for a headache, then a cold sachet for the catarrh, and have taken a double dose without knowing.

  • Read the packet of every sachet and tablet you take, even the small sachet you buy for a few hundred naira, and look for paracetamol or acetaminophen.
  • Never take two products containing it at the same time.
  • Ask your doctor what daily limit is safe for you. For a liver under strain it is often lower than the number on the box.
  • Never take paracetamol and alcohol together. This combination has put people in intensive care.

4. The mould you cannot always see

Groundnut, maize, egusi, ogiri, dried yam and melon seeds can grow a mould that makes a poison called aflatoxin. You can’t taste it or smell it in the soup, and frying, roasting or boiling won’t destroy it. That’s what makes it so dangerous.

This one matters more for you than for your neighbour. Aflatoxin and hepatitis B together are far worse than either one alone. In the long-running studies that showed this, carrying the virus raised the risk of liver cancer about eight times on its own, but carrying the virus and having aflatoxin in the body raised it about sixty times. Somebody without the virus can eat the same bad groundnut and be far less affected. There’s no need to panic, but it does mean a sealed container is worth more to you than to almost anyone else in your house. The good news is that this risk is almost entirely avoidable.

16
  • Buy groundnuts and maize you can see. Loose, unsorted, cheap sacks are the highest risk.
  • Throw away anything shrivelled, discoloured, dusty, or smelling musty. Don’t pick out the good ones and keep them, because the poison spreads beyond the nuts that look bad.
  • Be careful with roadside ground pepper and ground melon mixes. You cannot inspect what has already been ground.
  • Store dry, sealed and off the floor. Damp is what grows the mould in the first place.

5. The weight around your middle

Fat stored inside the liver is a disease of its own, and when it sits on top of hepatitis B the scarring moves faster. Chapter 6 deals with this properly. For now, know that it belongs on this list.

6. Cigarettes

Smoking raises the risk of liver cancer on its own, separately from the virus. If you smoke, stopping belongs on your list too.

7. Supplements nobody prescribed

Iron tablets you were not told to take, very high-dose vitamin A, bodybuilding and "herbal energy" products, and anything sold as a "liver detox" or "liver booster". If a doctor did not recommend it for a reason they explained to you, it does not go in.

8. Blades that have touched someone else

The barber's clipper. The manicure set at the salon. A shared razor at home. An injection given by someone who is not a health worker. This is one of the main ways hepatitis B still moves through Nigerian families, and Chapter 9 covers what to do about it.

Chapter five

05

Nigerian foods for a working liver

Before we get to the list, I want to be clear: no food clears hepatitis B. That includes bitter leaf, lemon in warm water, and every tea you have been told about.

What food can do is change how much extra work your liver has on top of what it already has. That matters a lot, even though it isn’t a cure, and anybody who tells you food is a cure is trying to sell you something.

34

The mistake almost everybody makes

The moment a Nigerian hears “liver problem”, the first thing that leaves the plate is meat and fish. Somebody's aunty said protein is heavy for the liver. So the person begins eating swallow and soup with no fish inside it, and two months later they are looking thin and weak and calling it healing.

Ah, this is backwards. A liver under strain needs protein to repair itself. Cutting it makes you lose muscle and slows down the repair you are hoping for.

There is one exception, and it only applies to people with advanced liver disease who have been given specific instructions by a doctor. If nobody has told you to restrict protein, do not restrict protein.

What a good plate looks like

Look at what changed. Nothing unusual was added. There are more vegetables, the protein is still there, and the swallow has gone from a mountain to a normal portion. That’s most of what this book asks of you at mealtimes.

Coffee, of all things

One of the more surprising findings in liver research is that regular coffee drinkers tend to have less scarring and less liver cancer than people who drink none. The evidence is observational rather than proof, so nobody should start drinking coffee as treatment. But if you already drink one or two cups of black coffee a day, there is no reason to stop, and some reason to continue.

Take it black. The sugar and the Peak milk don’t come with the benefit.

In short

Eat enough protein and plenty of vegetables, cut the swallow down, drink water, and check your grain. None of this treats the virus, but all of it makes your liver’s work easier.

36

The 14-day plan, and then you repeat it

This plan runs for fourteen days and then starts again. Hepatitis B stays with you for years, so the plan is two weeks of ordinary Nigerian food that you can go round and round for as long as you like. Day 15 is Day 1 again.

What this plan does

None of these meals will touch the virus. What they work on is the second disease, which is fat stored inside the liver. It sits on top of hepatitis B and makes the scarring move faster. You can change this one, and your waist measurement is how you keep track of it.

Three rules for all 14 days

  • Protein at every meal. Fish, egg, beans, chicken. Never a plate of swallow alone.
  • Swallow goes on the side. Take half of what you used to.
  • Between meals: cucumber, pawpaw, orange, guava, or groundnut you inspected yourself. Drink water all day, and no soft drinks, malt drinks or alcohol.

About the quantities you are about to read

Use them as a guide. The one cup, one ball and two pieces are there to show you what a good plate looks like: vegetables taking up the most room, a real piece of protein, and a small portion of swallow. Somebody doing hard physical work needs more than what is written here, and a small person who sits all day needs less.

The protein is the part you shouldn’t cut. European liver guidelines now say that cutting protein in liver disease is actively harmful, except in two rare hospital situations. For people with established liver damage they set the target at about one and a half grams for every kilogram you weigh, every day, which is far more than most people reading this are eating. You don’t need to weigh your food. A real piece of protein on all three plates will get you most of the way there.

If you have been given specific instructions by a doctor who has seen your results, those instructions win over anything printed here.

Chapter six

06

Weight, movement and the second disease

There is a second liver disease in Nigeria that almost nobody has been told about, and a great many people have it without knowing: fat stored inside the liver itself.

On its own it is a slow problem, but on top of hepatitis B it speeds the damage up. Two people with identical virus results can scar at very different speeds, and belly fat is one of the biggest reasons why.

This is also the chapter with the best news in the book, because this one is almost entirely in your hands. You don’t need a prescription, an appointment or any money to work on it.

40

Measure your waist, not your weight

The bathroom scale won’t tell you what you need to know. Two people can weigh the same and carry completely different amounts of the fat that matters, which is the fat around the organs in your middle.

Take a tape measure. Stand normally, breathe out, and measure around your waist at the level of your navel. Write the number in the log. Measure again once a month, on the same morning of the month, before breakfast.

If that number is going down, you are doing well, even in a month when the scale hasn’t moved at all.

The movement that works

  • Brisk walking, thirty minutes, five days a week. Brisk means you could still talk but would rather not sing. This alone reduces liver fat.
  • A short walk after your largest meal. Ten minutes is enough, and it’s the easiest habit in the book to keep.
  • Something that makes your big muscles work, twice a week. Squats holding a chair, carrying water, climbing stairs. Muscle is where your body stores sugar it would otherwise store as fat.

You don’t have to enjoy it or be good at it. The hard part is doing it on the days you don’t feel like it.

If you are already slim

Do not skip this chapter. It is possible to be thin and still carry fat inside the liver, particularly if your diet is mostly swallow and rice with little protein. The waist measurement and the walking still apply to you.

41

Measure your waist at the navel, before breakfast, on the same morning each week, and write it in the log. That number is how you keep score, because the bathroom scale hides the fat that matters.

Chapter seven

07

Who to tell, and how to sleep again

Ask anybody living with hepatitis B in Nigeria what the hardest part is, and almost none of them will say the virus.

They will tell you it’s the silence: carrying something you can’t mention, hearing a colleague talk about somebody who "has hepatitis" and saying nothing, and wondering whether the person you love will stay.

No blood test measures this and few doctors ask about it, but it can spoil more of your days than the virus does. That’s why it has its own chapter.

You don’t have to tell everybody

This is your private medical business, and it belongs to you. You are not obliged to tell your employer, your landlord, your church, your friends or your extended family. Your neighbour doesn’t need to know, your colleague doesn’t need to know, and that aunty who carries news from house to house especially doesn’t need to know.

Three groups do need to know: anyone you are sexually involved with, anyone you live with closely enough to share a razor or a toothbrush, and any doctor, dentist or nurse treating you. For everyone else it’s your choice, and keeping it private is not lying.

44

The conversation you are dreading

Telling a partner is the one that keeps people awake at night, so let’s go through it step by step. There are four things to say, in this order:

  1. What it is. "I have hepatitis B. It is a virus in the liver. I have had it a long time, most likely since I was a child."
  2. What is being done. "I am under a doctor. I have my numbers. I am being monitored properly."
  3. What it means for them. "You need a test. If you are negative, there is a vaccine, and after it you are protected."
  4. What you need. "I needed you to know. I am not asking you to fix anything."

That fourth point matters more than the other three. I know the fear. You are lying down at night thinking, what if this person leaves me? That fear is normal. But hear me well: most people panic because they think they are being handed a problem. When you hand them a plan instead, you will see the whole conversation change in front of you.

Tell them this

A vaccinated partner is a protected partner. Once they complete the vaccine and it has worked, they cannot catch it from you. Couples live full lives with this, have children, and grow old together. It happens all the time.

If they react badly

Some do, at first. Usually it’s fear coming out as anger, and it passes once they understand that they can be protected. Give them a few days and show them this page. If somebody leaves over something you were brave enough to share, that tells you something about them.

45

The heavy weeks

In the first months after a diagnosis, many people go through a stretch that looks like this: lying awake, searching the same thing on the phone at 2am, snapping at people, losing interest in things, feeling like a burden, imagining a future that is much worse than the likely one.

It happens to almost anybody who gets news like this. It doesn’t mean you are weak, and it does get better.

  • Stop Googling at night. At 2am the most frightening page always seems to come up first, and it is rarely the one that applies to you.
  • Give worry a fixed time. Fifteen minutes in the afternoon, written down. Outside that, it waits.
  • Tell your one person when it is a bad week. That is what Week 8 was for.
  • If it lasts more than two weeks, or you can’t sleep, can’t eat, or stop wanting to be here, tell a doctor. That is a medical problem with a medical answer, just like the virus.

Something to say in the morning

These aren’t about being healed. They are about the things you can control.

"I found out early, and that is an advantage."

"I know my numbers. Most people do not."

"My household is protected because of what I did."

"I am doing the things that are mine to do."

The scan you keep having when nothing is wrong

Every six months, for life: an ultrasound of your liver, usually with a blood test alongside it.

It will feel pointless. You will feel perfectly well, the scan will come back normal again, and by the third or fourth time you will be asking yourself, am I not just wasting money here? Everybody asks that. Ask it, then go and do the scan anyway.

What this scan looks for can be treated when it is small and found early, and is very hard to treat once it is big enough to hurt. That’s why the scan happens on a schedule. By the time there is a symptom, the easy window has closed.

The scan you feel like skipping is the one that protects you.

The eight questions

Ask them out loud. You don’t need to copy them down, because they are printed again at the back of this book under What my doctor said, with room to write the answer under each one.

  1. What is my viral load, and has it changed since last time?
  2. What is my ALT?
  3. Do I have any scarring, and how was that measured?
  4. Do I need treatment now? If not, what would change that?
  5. When is my next ultrasound due?
  6. Is everything I am taking safe for my liver, including what I buy myself?
  7. Has everyone in my household been tested and vaccinated?
  8. What should make me come back before my next appointment?

When you cannot afford it this month

This happens to a lot of people. Tests cost money, appointments cost transport, and the six-month scan comes round whether or not the month has been kind.

The worst thing you can do is disappear. Here’s what to do instead:

  • Tell the doctor plainly. "I can afford one test this month. Which one?" This is a normal question and they will answer it.
  • Ask about the free score. A reasonable estimate of scarring can be worked out from ordinary blood results you may already have. It costs nothing extra.
  • Ask about teaching hospitals and government facilities, which are usually cheaper than private laboratories for the same test.
  • Ask about the generic tablet. If you are on treatment, generic versions are far cheaper and are the same medicine. Ask before you ration or stop.
  • Never stretch a tablet by taking it every other day. It can make the virus harder to control later. Talk to the doctor instead.
  • Check whether health insurance covers it. Many people are enrolled through work and have never checked what it includes.

What really costs money

Everything on this page costs far less than a hospital admission after five years of not checking. That’s why the scan is worth fighting for, even in a tight month.

Protecting everyone else

This is the chapter to forward. If you do nothing else with this book, send this chapter to your brothers and sisters. They are far more likely to be carrying this virus than most people, and far more likely not to know it.

Figure 4: how it spreads, and how it does not

It can spread this way

  • Mother to baby at birth
  • Sex without protection
  • Shared razors and clippers
  • Shared toothbrush
  • Unsterile needles or injections
  • Manicure and pedicure tools
  • Unscreened blood

It cannot spread this way

  • Eating from the same bowl
  • Sharing cups and cutlery
  • Hugging, kissing, holding hands
  • Coughing or sneezing
  • Toilets and bathrooms
  • Mosquito bites
  • Cooking for your family

Read that second column twice, and then read it out loud to anybody who starts behaving somehow. You can’t get hepatitis B from eating somebody’s food, from greeting them, or from mosquito. Almost all the wickedness people with this virus suffer in this country comes from people who believe that second column belongs in the first one.

The vaccine that can end this in your family

Hepatitis B is one of the very few serious viruses in the world with an excellent vaccine. It takes three injections, and then your husband or wife and your children are covered for years, and most of them for life. It isn’t expensive, and ordinary clinics have it.

Figure 5: the schedule

Don’t assume the vaccine worked. Ask for the blood test afterwards that confirms it. A small number of people need a repeat course, and it is better to know.

56

If you are pregnant, or may become pregnant

This is the most urgent page in the book for anyone it applies to, because the window is measured in hours.

  • Tell the clinic you are hepatitis B positive at your first antenatal visit. Don’t leave it till later, because it changes the plan for the birth.
  • The baby needs the first vaccine dose within 24 hours of being born. Say this out loud in the delivery room. Say it more than once.
  • Ask whether the additional injection that gives immediate protection is available where you will deliver, and arrange it in advance if it is not.
  • Ask about having your viral load checked during pregnancy. If it is high, a tablet in the last months of pregnancy substantially lowers the risk of passing it on.
  • Breastfeeding is generally considered safe once the baby has been vaccinated. Ask your own doctor about your own situation.

Why this page matters so much

Most Nigerians carrying this virus got it as newborns. Everything on this page is there to stop that happening to your baby. When the birth is handled properly, the virus stops with you.

57

Ordinary things, handled well

  • Your own clipper. Buy a set and take it to the barber, or find a barber who sterilises properly. This applies to your sons too.
  • Your own manicure set at the salon.
  • Your own razor and toothbrush, kept separately at home.
  • Cover any cut with a plaster until it has healed, and clean up your own blood with bleach and water.
  • Never take an injection from anybody who is not a health worker, and never let a needle be reused.
  • Tell every dentist and doctor before they treat you, so they know what to watch for.

What you do not have to do

You don’t need separate plates, and you don’t need to stop cooking for your family. There’s no need to eat alone, avoid hugging your children, or sit apart at gatherings. None of that protects anybody, and all of it hurts.

You found out in time, and now you can protect the people you love.
