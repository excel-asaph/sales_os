Before anything else

5 things you have probably been told that are not true

Almost everybody handed this diagnosis in Nigeria is handed these five along with it — usually by somebody who meant well. Every one of them is wrong, and believing any of them costs you something real.

Myth 1

“If I feel fine, it cannot be serious.”

The truth

Feeling fine is the symptom.

Your liver has no pain nerves inside it. It can lose a great deal of its working tissue and say absolutely nothing. By the time somebody’s eyes turn yellow, years have already gone. Almost everybody who ends up in real trouble with this virus felt perfectly well for most of the journey there.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 1.

Myth 2

“Somebody’s herbs can flush it out.”

The truth

Nothing flushes it out — and some of those bottles are doing the damage themselves.

No herb, no tea, no root has ever been shown to clear hepatitis B from a human body. Think about it small: if one of them worked, would it still be in an unlabelled bottle on a table by the roadside? And it is worse than useless. Herbal preparations are a recognised cause of liver injury, and the organ that has to process that mixture is the one already carrying a virus.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

Myth 3

“This means I am going to die soon.”

The truth

Most people living with hepatitis B die of something else entirely, decades later.

What decides your outcome is not the virus. It is whether anybody is watching it. Checked properly, and treated in the years when treatment is actually needed, most people carry this for a full lifetime and it never becomes the thing that ends it.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 2.

Myth 4

“People can catch it from my food, my cup, my greeting.”

The truth

They cannot. Not from food, not from cups, not from hugging, not from mosquito.

It travels through blood, and from mother to baby at birth. It does not travel through a shared plate of rice. Almost all of the wickedness people with this virus suffer in this country comes from neighbours who believe otherwise. You do not need separate plates. You do not need to eat alone. You do not need to stop cooking for your own family.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 9.

Myth 5

“There is nothing anybody can do anyway.”

The truth

There is a great deal, and most of it is cheap.

Three injections protect everyone living in your house, for life. Taking alcohol out does more to slow the damage than any food ever will. A scan every six months finds trouble while it is still small and still treatable. None of that is exotic, none of it is expensive, and all of it is yours to do.

Dealt with properly in Chapters 4 and 9.

If you recognised even one of them, you are in exactly the right place — and you have not lost anything yet. Keep reading.

About this book

You are reading this because of a piece of paper.

Maybe it came from a test before a wedding. Maybe you went to donate blood and somebody called you aside. Maybe you were pregnant, and the nurse's face changed while she was reading.

However it reached you, one thing is true this morning: you feel completely fine, and you have been told that you are not.

That is the strangest part of it. No pain. No fever. Nothing at all. Just a word on a result sheet — reactive — and then a silence that nobody filled in for you. You were handed the paper. You were not handed an explanation. If nobody sat you down and explained, you are not alone — that is how it goes in most places. They give you the result, they call the next person.

So let us fill in the silence.

iii

What nobody said to you that day

You are not dying. Not this year. Most likely not for a very long time, and possibly not from this at all.

About one in every twelve Nigerian adults is carrying exactly what you are carrying. Almost none of them know. They will find out in ten years, or fifteen, when something finally starts to hurt — and by then their choices will be fewer than yours are today.

You found out early. That sounds like the worst day of your life. It is actually the best advantage anybody with this virus can have, and this book is about spending it well.

Inside this book

  • The tests that show what is truly happening in your liver — and the ones your first hospital visit almost certainly skipped
  • Why you feel perfectly healthy, and why that is the most dangerous thing about this virus
  • The everyday Nigerian foods quietly damaging your liver while you are trying to protect it
  • The mixtures sold to "flush" hepatitis from your body — and why the people selling them are the biggest danger you face this year
  • How to protect your husband, your wife, your children, your brothers and sisters — starting this week
  • What to say, who to say it to, and what you never have to explain to anybody

What this book will not do

It will not tell you that hepatitis B can be washed out of your body by food, by herbs, by hot water, by bitter roots, or by prayer alone. It cannot. Anybody charging you money for that is charging you for a story.

Some of them will do worse than take your money. A number of the mixtures sold for "liver cleansing" are hard on the liver itself — and yours is the organ already under strain. We come back to this in Chapter 3, because it matters more than anything else in these pages.

Here is what can be done. This virus can be watched properly. It can be held down with medicine when medicine is needed. Damage that has already started can often ease once the virus is under control. And the people sleeping in your house tonight can be protected completely, this month, for less than you think.

That is not a small promise. It is simply a true one.

Chapter one

01

What just happened

Reactive. Positive. HBsAg +ve. Three different ways of writing the same sentence: there is a virus living in your liver, and it has been there a long while.

Probably far longer than you think. Most Nigerians carrying hepatitis B did not catch it last year from anything they did. They got it as a baby or a small child — at birth, or from a cut, a shared blade, an injection given in good faith. It moved in quietly and has been sitting there ever since, doing very little, waiting.

This matters because of what it means about you. You did nothing wrong. Not then, and not now. So put that guilt down. Leave it right there on the floor where you are standing. You will need your strength for other things.

Your liver, in one paragraph

Everything you swallow goes to your liver first. Food, drink, medicine, whatever the chemist handed you last week — all of it passes through before it reaches the rest of your body. Your liver decides what gets in and what gets thrown out. It stores your energy. It makes the substance that stops a cut from bleeding forever. It is the busiest organ you own.

It is also the only one that can rebuild itself after damage. Hold on to that sentence. It is the reason this book exists.

Why you feel fine, and why that is the problem

Your liver cannot feel pain. There are no pain nerves inside it — only in the thin covering around it, and that covering does not complain until things have gone seriously far.

So a liver can lose a great deal of its working tissue and still say nothing. No warning. No symptom. Nothing you would take to a doctor. By the time the yellow shows in somebody's eyes, years have already passed, and the conversation in the consulting room has changed completely.

This is why hepatitis B is called a silent disease. Not because it is gentle — because it is quiet. You are feeling fine. Fine well well. And that, my friend, is exactly the trouble.

Feeling well is not evidence. It is the one symptom this virus reliably produces.

The long road, and where you are standing

Damage to a liver does not happen in one night. It happens in stages, over years, and each stage is quieter than it deserves to be.

Figure 1 — the road, and where people usually notice

Look at that picture again. The whole left side of it — years of it — feels like nothing. That is not a warning to frighten you. It is the reason this book is built around ninety days instead of ninety symptoms. Nothing is going to tap you on the shoulder.

Two words you will hear

Acute means the virus arrived recently. Most healthy adults who catch it as adults fight it off within six months and are finished with it for life.

Chronic means it has stayed longer than six months. If you caught it as a baby, it is almost certainly chronic — a young immune system does not know to fight it. This is the situation most Nigerian adults are in, and it is the situation this book is written for.

Chapter two

02

The two questions that decide everything

Two people can walk into the same clinic on the same morning with the same result, and walk out into two completely different lives.

One is told to come back in six months. The other is started on a tablet she will take every day for many years. Neither of them is being treated badly. They are simply in different places, and two questions decide which.

Question one: is the virus active?

A quiet virus and a busy virus need different things. Three results answer this together — your ALT, your viral load, and HBeAg. No single one of them decides anything on its own, which is why a doctor who has only ordered one of them has not finished the job.

Question two: has it already caused scarring?

This is the question almost nobody asks, and it is the more important of the two. Scarring — doctors call it fibrosis — is the liver slowly replacing working tissue with something more like a callus.

It can be measured two ways. A special scan presses gently on the liver and measures how stiff it has become; stiff means scarred. Or a score can be worked out from blood tests you may already have had, which costs nothing extra and is a reasonable place to start if money is tight.

The four rooms

Put those answers together and you land in one of four situations. Doctors have long names for them. Here is what they actually mean.

Figure 2 — the four situations

The room nobody warns you about

Look at the bottom right. A liver that has been settled and harmless for fifteen years can wake up again, quietly, with no symptom to announce it. This is the single best argument for keeping your six-month appointments even in the years when you feel wonderful and nothing has ever gone wrong.

8

If you are told you need medicine

It is usually one tablet, once a day. The two most commonly used are tenofovir and entecavir. Both are very good at what they do.

Here is what they do, stated honestly. They hold the virus down — often so far down it cannot be found in your blood at all. They stop the ongoing injury. And when the injury stops, the liver gets its chance to do the thing only a liver can do: repair. Scarring that has already formed often eases once the virus has been suppressed for long enough. Not always, and not overnight. But often.

Here is what they do not do. They do not remove the virus from your body. It stays, quietly, in the background. That is why the tablet is usually long-term, and often lifelong.

Never stop the tablet on your own

This is the most important safety sentence in this book. Stopping hepatitis B medicine suddenly can let the virus come roaring back, and that rebound can do serious harm quickly — sometimes worse than anything that came before.

People stop for ordinary reasons. Money ran out. They felt fine. Somebody at church said they had been healed. If any of those is about to happen to you, tell your doctor first and let them plan it with you. There are cheaper options and there are ways to stop safely. Disappearing is not one of them.

If you are told you do not need medicine yet

This is good news and it is not a discharge. "Watch" means blood tests once or twice a year and a scan every six months, forever. The whole value of being watched is that the moment anything changes, you are already in the room.

Being watched is not being ignored. It is having someone looking, on a schedule, at the thing that will not announce itself.

Chapter three

03

The silent enemies

Your liver is already carrying something. Everything in this chapter adds a second weight on top of it — and unlike the virus, every one of these is yours to put down.

Read this chapter twice. If you only act on one chapter in this whole book, it should be this one, because avoiding harm is faster and cheaper than repairing it.

1. Alcohol

Let me not waste your time: the number is zero.

Not "reduce it". Not "only at weekends". Not "only beer". Alcohol and hepatitis B do not simply add together — they multiply, and the liver that receives both scars faster than either would cause alone.

And before you start forming an exception in your mind — yes, beer is alcohol. Palm wine is alcohol. Those bitters they advertise as good for men, sold in the small bottle at the junction, are alcohol. I know somebody is reading this and already choosing which one does not count. Drop it. If you drink, this is the single most valuable change available to you, and it is worth more than every food in Chapter 5 combined.

14

2. The mixtures sold to cure you

Somebody has already offered you one. Possibly this morning.

Shine your eyes here. Of everything in this book, this is the page most likely to save your life.

Agbo. A bottle from a trusted neighbour. A powder from a man on the radio. A tea imported in a tin, with a testimony attached. They all promise the same thing in the same words — that this will flush it out, wash the liver, clear it completely.

Two things are true about these, and the second one is worse than the first.

The first is that they do not work. No herb, no tea, no root, no mixture has ever been shown to clear hepatitis B from a human body. Think about it small: if one of them worked, would it still be in an unlabelled bottle on a table by the roadside? It would be in every hospital in the world before the year finished.

The second is that some of them cause real harm. Herbal preparations are a recognised cause of liver injury, and in this part of the world they are a documented reason people arrive in hospital with livers failing. The problem is not that herbs are evil. The problem is that nobody can tell you what is in an unlabelled bottle, how much of it, or how strong this week's batch is compared to last week's.

And somebody will tell you their uncle drank something and it cleared completely. Ask that person one simple question: “Please, can I see the test result before, and the test result after?” Then wait. You will be waiting for a long time.

Your liver is the organ that has to process every one of those substances. It is also the organ already carrying a virus. Ask it to do both and you are running an experiment on the one part of your body you cannot replace.

The hardest sentence in this book

If you have been taking something like this, you have not ruined anything. Stop it, tell your doctor exactly what you were taking — take the bottle with you if you still have it — and ask for a liver function test. That is the whole repair. Nobody is going to shout at you.

15

3. Paracetamol, and the trap inside it

Paracetamol is in almost every house in Nigeria and it is not the villain here. Taken properly it is one of the safer painkillers for a liver.

The trap is that it hides. It is inside many cold-and-catarrh sachets, many flu tablets, many "extra strength" pain mixtures, often under a different name on the packet. People take a paracetamol for a headache, then a cold sachet for the catarrh, and have taken a double dose without knowing.

  • Read the packet of every sachet and tablet you take — yes, even the small sachet you buy for a few hundred naira — and look for paracetamol or acetaminophen.
  • Never take two products containing it at the same time.
  • Ask your doctor what daily limit applies to you — for a liver under strain it is often lower than the number on the box.
  • Never take paracetamol and alcohol together. This combination has put people in intensive care.

4. The mould you cannot always see

Groundnut, maize, egusi, ogiri, dried yam and melon seeds can grow a mould that produces a poison called aflatoxin. You cannot taste it. You cannot smell it in the soup. Frying does not kill it. Roasting does not kill it. Boiling does not kill it. That is exactly what makes it dangerous — it never announces itself.

On its own it is bad for a liver. On top of hepatitis B it is much worse than bad — the two together raise the risk of liver cancer far more than either does alone. This is one of the strongest reasons this chapter exists, and it is almost entirely avoidable.

16
  • Buy groundnuts and maize you can see. Loose, unsorted, cheap sacks are the highest risk.
  • Throw away anything shrivelled, discoloured, dusty, or smelling musty. Do not pick out the good ones and keep them — the poison spreads beyond the visibly bad nut.
  • Be careful with roadside ground pepper and ground melon mixes. You cannot inspect what has already been ground.
  • Store dry, sealed and off the floor. Damp is what grows the mould in the first place.

5. The weight around your middle

Fat stored inside the liver is a disease of its own, and when it sits on top of hepatitis B the scarring moves faster. Chapter 6 deals with this properly. For now, know that it belongs on this list.

6. Cigarettes

Smoking raises liver cancer risk by itself, separately from the virus. There is no version of this book in which smoking is acceptable.

7. Supplements nobody prescribed

Iron tablets you were not told to take, very high-dose vitamin A, bodybuilding and "herbal energy" products, and anything sold as a "liver detox" or "liver booster". If a doctor did not recommend it for a reason they explained to you, it does not go in.

8. Blades that have touched someone else

The barber's clipper. The manicure set at the salon. A shared razor at home. An injection given by someone who is not a health worker. This is one of the main ways hepatitis B still moves through Nigerian families, and Chapter 9 covers what to do about it.

Chapter five

05

Nigerian foods for a working liver

Let us be honest with each other before the list starts: no food clears hepatitis B. Not bitter leaf. Not lemon and warm water. Not any tea.

What food does is decide how much extra work your liver has to do on top of the work it already has. That is not a small thing. It is simply a different thing from a cure, and anybody who blurs the two is selling something.

34

The mistake almost everybody makes

The moment a Nigerian hears “liver problem”, the first thing that leaves the plate is meat and fish. Somebody's aunty said protein is heavy for the liver. So the person begins eating swallow and soup with no fish inside it, and two months later they are looking thin and weak and calling it healing.

Ah. This is backwards. A liver under strain needs protein to repair itself. Cutting it makes you lose muscle and slows the very repair you are trying to support.

There is one exception, and it only applies to people with advanced liver disease who have been given specific instructions by a doctor. If nobody has told you to restrict protein, do not restrict protein.

What a plate actually looks like

Notice what changed. Nothing exotic was added. The vegetables grew, the protein stayed, and the mound of swallow became a portion instead of a mountain. That is the entire dietary instruction in this book.

Coffee, of all things

One of the more surprising findings in liver research is that regular coffee drinkers tend to have less scarring and less liver cancer than people who drink none. The evidence is observational rather than proof, so nobody should start drinking coffee as treatment. But if you already drink one or two cups of black coffee a day, there is no reason to stop, and some reason to continue.

Black. The sugar and the condensed milk are not part of the finding.

The honest summary

Eat enough protein. Eat plenty of vegetables. Cut the swallow down. Drink water. Inspect your grain. Nothing on this page is a treatment, and all of it makes your liver's day easier.

36

The 14-day plan — and then you repeat it

Fourteen days, not ten, and it does not end. Hepatitis B is not a fortnight’s problem, so this is not a fortnight’s diet — it is two weeks of ordinary Nigerian food that you rotate for as long as you like. Day 15 is Day 1 again.

What this plan is actually doing

Not one meal here touches the virus. What it treats is the second disease — fat stored inside the liver, which sits on top of hepatitis B and makes the scarring move faster. That one is yours to change, and your waist measurement is how you watch it.

Three rules for all 14 days

  • Protein at every meal. Fish, egg, beans, chicken. Never a plate of swallow alone.
  • Swallow is the side, not the meal. Half what you used to take.
  • Between meals: cucumber, pawpaw, orange, guava, or groundnut you inspected yourself. Water throughout — no soft drinks, no malt, no alcohol.

Chapter six

06

Weight, movement and the second disease

There is a second liver disease in Nigeria that almost nobody has been told about, and a great many people have it without knowing: fat stored inside the liver itself.

On its own it is a slow problem. Sitting on top of hepatitis B, it is an accelerator. Two people with identical virus results can scar at very different speeds, and belly fat is one of the biggest reasons why.

This is also the chapter with the best news in the book, because this one is almost entirely in your hands. No prescription. No appointment. No money.

40

Measure your waist, not your weight

The scale is the wrong instrument. Two people can weigh the same and carry completely different amounts of the fat that matters, which is the fat around the organs in your middle.

Take a tape measure. Stand normally, breathe out, and measure around your waist at the level of your navel. Write the number in the log. Measure again once a month, on the same morning of the month, before breakfast.

If that number is going down, you are winning — even in a month when the scale has not moved at all.

The movement that actually works

  • Brisk walking, thirty minutes, five days a week. Brisk means you could still talk but would rather not sing. This alone reduces liver fat.
  • A short walk after your largest meal. Ten minutes. Easiest habit in the book to keep.
  • Something that makes your big muscles work, twice a week. Squats holding a chair, carrying water, climbing stairs. Muscle is where your body stores sugar it would otherwise store as fat.

You do not need to enjoy it and you do not need to be good at it. You need to do it on days when you do not feel like it, which is the only part that is actually difficult.

If you are already slim

Do not skip this chapter. It is possible to be thin and still carry fat inside the liver, particularly if your diet is mostly swallow and rice with little protein. The waist measurement and the walking still apply to you.

41

Chapter seven

07

Who to tell, and how to sleep again

Ask anybody living with hepatitis B in Nigeria what the hardest part is, and almost none of them will say the virus.

They will say the silence. Carrying something you cannot mention. Watching a colleague talk about somebody who "has hepatitis" and saying nothing. Wondering whether the person you love will stay.

No blood test measures this, no doctor asks about it, and it does more damage to more days than the virus does. So it gets a chapter.

You do not owe everybody the truth

This is your private medical business. It belongs to you and nobody else. You are not obliged to tell your employer, your landlord, your church, your friends or your extended family. Your neighbour does not need to know. Your colleague does not need to know. And that aunty who carries news from house to house — she especially does not need to know.

Three groups genuinely need to know: anyone you are sexually involved with, anyone you live with closely enough to share a razor or a toothbrush, and any doctor, dentist or nurse treating you. That is the list. Everyone else is your choice, and choosing no is not dishonesty.

44

The conversation you are dreading

Telling a partner is the one that keeps people awake, so let us make it concrete. There are four things to say, in this order:

  1. What it is. "I have hepatitis B. It is a virus in the liver. I have had it a long time, most likely since I was a child."
  2. What is being done. "I am under a doctor. I have my numbers. I am being monitored properly."
  3. What it means for them. "You need a test. If you are negative, there is a vaccine, and after it you are protected."
  4. What you need. "I needed you to know. I am not asking you to fix anything."

That fourth point matters more than the other three. I know the fear — you are lying down at night thinking, what if this person leaves me? That fear is normal. But hear me well: most people panic because they think they are being handed a problem. Hand them a plan instead, and watch the whole conversation change in front of you.

The sentence that changes the whole conversation

A vaccinated partner is a protected partner. Once they complete the vaccine and it has worked, they cannot catch it from you. Couples live full lives with this, have children, and grow old together. This is ordinary, not exceptional.

If they react badly

Some do, at first. It is usually fear wearing the face of anger, and it usually passes once they understand that they can be protected. Give them a few days and the facts on this page. If somebody leaves over information you were brave enough to share, they were telling you something about themselves.

45

The heavy weeks

In the first months after a diagnosis, many people go through a stretch that looks like this: lying awake, searching the same thing on the phone at 2am, snapping at people, losing interest in things, feeling like a burden, imagining a future that is much worse than the likely one.

That is not weakness and it is not your liver. It is what happens to almost anybody handed news like this, and it lifts.

  • Stop Googling at night. Nothing you find at 2am is true, and the most frightening page is always the one that comes first.
  • Give worry a fixed time. Fifteen minutes in the afternoon, written down. Outside that, it waits.
  • Tell your one person when it is a bad week. That is what Week 8 was for.
  • If it lasts more than two weeks — if you cannot sleep, cannot eat, or stop wanting to be here — tell a doctor. That is a medical problem with a medical answer, exactly like the virus.

Something to say in the morning

Not affirmations about being healed — you already know this book does not trade in that. These are about the only thing that was ever in your control.

"I found out early, and that is an advantage."

"I know my numbers. Most people do not."

"My household is protected because of what I did."

"I am doing the things that are mine to do."

The scan you keep having when nothing is wrong

Every six months, for life: an ultrasound of your liver, usually with a blood test alongside it.

This will feel pointless. You will feel perfectly well. The scan will come back normal, again, and by the third or fourth time you will be asking yourself, am I not just wasting money here? Every single person asks that question. Ask it, then go and do the scan.

Here is why. The thing this scan looks for is treatable when it is small and found by accident, and very difficult when it is large and found because it started to hurt. The entire value of the scan is that it happens on a schedule rather than in response to a symptom — because by the time there is a symptom, the easy window has closed.

The scan you resent paying for is the one doing the actual work.

The eight questions

Copy these onto paper and take them in. Ask them out loud.

  1. What is my viral load, and has it changed since last time?
  2. What is my ALT?
  3. Do I have any scarring, and how was that measured?
  4. Do I need treatment now? If not, what exactly would change that?
  5. When is my next ultrasound due?
  6. Is everything I am taking safe for my liver — including what I buy myself?
  7. Has everyone in my household been tested and vaccinated?
  8. What should make me come back before my next appointment?

When you cannot afford it this month

This is real and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Tests cost money, appointments cost transport, and the six-month scan arrives whether or not the month has been kind.

What not to do is disappear. What to do instead:

  • Tell the doctor plainly. "I can afford one test this month. Which one?" This is a normal question and they will answer it.
  • Ask about the free score. A reasonable estimate of scarring can be worked out from ordinary blood results you may already have. It costs nothing extra.
  • Ask about teaching hospitals and government facilities, which are usually cheaper than private laboratories for the same test.
  • Ask about the generic tablet. If you are on treatment, generic versions are far cheaper and are the same medicine. Ask before you ration or stop.
  • Never stretch a tablet by taking it every other day. That is worse than useless — it can make the virus harder to control later. Talk to the doctor instead.
  • Check whether health insurance covers it. Many people are enrolled through work and have never checked what it includes.

The thing that actually costs money

Everything on this page is cheaper than the hospital admission that follows five years of not looking. That is not a scare — it is arithmetic, and it is why the scan is worth arguing for.

Protecting everyone else

This is the chapter to forward. If you do nothing else with this book, send this chapter to your brothers and sisters, because they are far more likely to be carrying this than the general population — and far more likely not to know.

Figure 4 — how it spreads, and how it does not

It can spread this way

  • Mother to baby at birth
  • Sex without protection
  • Shared razors and clippers
  • Shared toothbrush
  • Unsterile needles or injections
  • Manicure and pedicure tools
  • Unscreened blood

It cannot spread this way

  • Eating from the same bowl
  • Sharing cups and cutlery
  • Hugging, kissing, holding hands
  • Coughing or sneezing
  • Toilets and bathrooms
  • Mosquito bites
  • Cooking for your family

Read that second column twice, and then read it out loud to anybody who starts behaving somehow. You cannot get hepatitis B from eating somebody's food. You cannot get it from greeting them. You cannot get it from mosquito. Almost all the wickedness people with this virus suffer in this country comes from people who believe that second column belongs in the first one.

55

The vaccine, which actually ends this

Hepatitis B is one of the very few serious viruses in the world with a genuinely excellent vaccine. Three injections. That is all. Three injections, and your husband, your wife, your children are covered — for years, and for most people for life. Not expensive, and available in ordinary clinics.

Figure 5 — the schedule

Do not assume the series worked — ask for the blood test afterwards that confirms it. A small number of people need a repeat course, and it is better to know.

56

If you are pregnant, or may become pregnant

This is the most urgent page in the book for anyone it applies to, because the window is measured in hours.

  • Tell the clinic you are hepatitis B positive at your first antenatal visit. Not later. This changes the plan for the birth.
  • The baby needs the first vaccine dose within 24 hours of being born. Say this out loud in the delivery room. Say it more than once.
  • Ask whether the additional injection that gives immediate protection is available where you will deliver, and arrange it in advance if it is not.
  • Ask about having your viral load checked during pregnancy. If it is high, a tablet in the last months of pregnancy substantially lowers the risk of passing it on.
  • Breastfeeding is generally considered safe once the baby has been vaccinated. Ask your own doctor about your own situation.

Why this page matters more than any other

Most Nigerians carrying this virus got it as newborns. Everything on this page exists to stop that happening one more time. A birth handled properly is a chain broken permanently.

Ordinary things, handled well

  • Your own clipper. Buy a set and take it to the barber, or find a barber who sterilises properly. This applies to your sons too.
  • Your own manicure set at the salon.
  • Your own razor and toothbrush, kept separately at home.
  • Cover any cut with a plaster until it has healed, and clean up your own blood with bleach and water.
  • Never take an injection from anybody who is not a health worker, and never let a needle be reused.
  • Tell every dentist and doctor before they treat you. Not because they will refuse — because it changes what they watch for.

What you do not have to do

You do not need separate plates. You do not need to stop cooking for your family. You do not need to eat alone, avoid hugging your children, or sit apart at gatherings. None of that protects anybody, and all of it hurts.

You are not a danger to the people you love. You are the person who found out in time to protect them.
