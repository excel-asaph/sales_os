Before anything else

5 things you have been told about losing weight that are costing you money

Four of these are sold to you. One of them is sold to you by yourself. All five are why the last attempt did not last.

Myth 1

“Carbohydrate is the enemy. I must stop swallow.”

The truth

Nobody in this country has ever kept that up, and you will not either.

Every plan that tells a Nigerian to stop eating eba, amala, rice and yam works for about three weeks and then collapses, because it was never a plan — it was a punishment with a deadline. This book does not remove a single food. It changes the order you eat them in, and the size of one thing on the plate.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 2.

Myth 2

“Flat tummy tea is natural, so it is safe.”

The truth

Most of it is laxative. Some of it has contained a drug banned worldwide for causing strokes.

What you lose on a laxative is water and the contents of your bowel. It returns the day you stop, because you never lost any fat. And “natural” is a word on a label, not a test result — slimming products bought in this region have repeatedly been found to contain sibutramine, which was pulled off the world market in 2010 after it caused heart attacks and strokes.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

Myth 3

“I am not losing weight, so nothing is working.”

The truth

The scale is the least useful number in this book.

In the first week your body dumps water and then holds some back, so the scale can sit still while your waist is falling. Muscle also weighs more than the fat replacing it. Use a tape measure. A waist that has gone down two centimetres is real progress even if the scale has not moved a gram.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 7.

Myth 4

“I must do heavy exercise or it will not count.”

The truth

You will lose far more in the kitchen than in the gym, and the walking is for keeping it off.

An hour of hard exercise burns roughly what one bottle of malt and a sausage roll put in. That is not an argument against moving — movement is what stops the weight coming back, and it protects the muscle you would otherwise lose. It is an argument against believing you can outrun your plate.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 6.

Myth 5

“My own is hereditary. It is my body, nothing works.”

The truth

Some of it genuinely is — and some of it is a thyroid nobody has checked.

Families do share shapes, and they also share one kitchen, one way of cooking and one idea of what a full plate looks like. But if your weight has climbed for no reason you can name, get your thyroid checked before you blame your discipline. No amount of willpower fixes a hormone, and it is a cheap test.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 8.

About this book

You have done this before. That is the first thing worth saying out loud.

You have started on a Monday. You have bought the tea. You have skipped dinner for two weeks and felt proud, then eaten everything in the house on a Saturday and felt like a failure. You have lost seven kilograms and found nine.

None of that was weakness. It was arithmetic. Every one of those plans asked you to stop eating the food you grew up on, and nobody keeps that up — not here, not anywhere.

What this book is

Ten switches, one a day, for ten days. Not ten rules and not ten sacrifices — ten exchanges. Each day you put one thing down and pick something else up, and the something else is food you already eat and already like.

That is what the 10X means. Ten exchanges. There is no pill in this book, no tea, no imported powder, and no machine.

Then you run them for ninety days. The ten days are how you switch everything on without being overwhelmed. The ninety are where your body actually changes — and Chapter 5 walks you through every week of them, including the one in week six where most people quietly give up.

What to expect, honestly

By Day 10: two to four centimetres off your waist, the bloating gone, and proof that your body still answers when you speak to it properly. Not a new size — a start.

By Day 90: somewhere between seven and thirteen kilograms, and seven to twelve centimetres off your waist. That is the point where other people start asking what you are doing.

Anything promising more than that, faster, is selling you water weight or a banned drug. Chapter 3 explains which.

Chapter one

01

Why Nigerian food is not the problem

Every diet book sold in this country starts by telling you that what your mother cooked is killing you. That is both insulting and wrong.

Eba is not the problem. Rice is not the problem. Amala, tuwo, yam, plantain and beans are not the problem. People have eaten all of them for generations without carrying this weight.

Four things changed, and none of them is the food itself.

One — the portion grew, and the soup shrank

Look at a plate honestly. Most Nigerian plates today are three quarters amala or rice, with a smear of soup on the side and one small piece of meat. That is not a balanced meal with too much carbohydrate; it is a plate of carbohydrate with a garnish.

Your plate is not wrong. It is upside down.

Two — the drinks

This is the biggest one and the one nobody counts. A bottle of malt carries more sugar than most people would ever eat in one sitting, and because it is liquid your body does not register it as food at all. You drink 250 calories in ninety seconds and feel exactly as hungry as before.

Three — the oil went up

Not palm oil itself — the amount. A pot of stew that used to take three spoons now takes a cup, and deep frying arrived in kitchens where nothing used to be deep fried.

Four — the moving stopped

A generation ago people walked to the market, walked to school, walked to the farm. Now it is keke, okada, and a chair for nine hours. Nothing about your food changed as much as this did.

Which is genuinely good news

Every one of those four is a habit, and habits can be swapped. Not one of them requires you to stop eating Nigerian food, buy anything imported, or join anything.

Why you lost it before and found it again

Because you did it by suffering, and suffering has a time limit.

Cutting your food in half works — for a while. Then two things happen at once. Your body, which cannot tell the difference between a diet and a famine, slows down to protect you. And your mind, which has been holding its breath for six weeks, finally exhales. The weight comes back, and it brings interest.

The way out is not more discipline. It is a plan that does not require any.

Every switch in this book is designed to be permanent. That is the only reason it will work where the others did not — you are not enduring anything, so there is nothing to go back from.

Measure your waist, never your weight

Get a tailor’s tape. They cost almost nothing.

Your weight includes water, food still inside you, and muscle. It swings two kilograms in a day for reasons that have nothing to do with fat, which is why the scale destroys more plans than biscuits do.

Your waist measures the fat that actually matters — the kind packed around your organs, which is the kind that causes diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease.

Where exactly to put the tape

The numbers to aim below

For a woman, a waist under 80cm. For a man, under 94cm. Measured at the navel, standing, first thing in the morning, breathing out normally — not sucked in.

The two that do half the work

If you only ever did two of these, do Switch 1 and Switch 3.

The bottle switch, because liquid sugar is the largest single thing most Nigerian adults are carrying that they have never once counted. And the size switch, because halving the amala takes a quarter of a meal away without removing anything from the table.

Together those two are worth more than every slimming tea ever sold in this country, and they cost nothing.

Why the order you eat in changes what food does to you

Switch 2 is the one people dismiss, and it is the most interesting thing in this book.

Eat a big plate of white rice on an empty stomach and your blood sugar climbs steeply, your body releases a large amount of insulin to bring it down, and insulin’s other job is storing fat. Eat the same rice after the fish and the vegetables, and the climb is far gentler — the protein and fibre slow everything leaving your stomach.

Same plate. Same quantity. Same day. Different result, because of the order.

The only three steps in switch 2

And there is a second effect you will feel before you believe the first: by the time you reach the swallow, you are already partly full, so you eat less of it without having decided to eat less of anything.

Try it at your next meal

Do not change what you are eating today. Just eat it in this order: water first, then the meat and vegetables, then the swallow last. Notice how much swallow is left on the plate.

Chapter three

03

Flat tummy tea, slimming pills and the waist trainer

You have almost certainly bought one of them. Nearly everybody reading this has, and there is no shame in it — they are sold by people who look exactly like the result you want.

But you paid for them, and you deserve to know what you actually bought.

What is in the tea

Open almost any flat tummy tea and you find the same family of ingredients: senna, cascara, aloe, sometimes a diuretic herb. Every one of those is a laxative or a water-pusher.

Here is what that means in practice. You drink it, you go to the toilet several times, you feel lighter, and the scale is down a kilogram the next morning. It feels like it worked.

Nothing left your body except water and stool. Not one gram of fat was touched. Everything comes back within about two days of stopping, which is precisely why the tea is sold in packs that never quite finish.

And it is not harmless

Taking laxatives regularly leaves the bowel lazy, so it stops working properly without them. It also strips potassium, which matters for your heart rhythm. People end up in hospital from this, and they never connect it to a tea they thought was herbal.

The drug that was banned worldwide, and where it turned up

In 2010, a weight-loss drug called sibutramine was pulled off the market in Europe, America and elsewhere. It worked — that was never the issue. It was withdrawn because it raised blood pressure and heart rate, and a large trial found it causing heart attacks and strokes.

Since then, sibutramine has repeatedly been found inside slimming capsules and teas sold as purely herbal — across West Africa, across Asia, in products bought online and in markets. Not declared on the label. Not on the ingredients list. In products whose whole selling point is that they are natural.

So understand the actual trade. The slimming capsule that genuinely seems to work may be working because there is a withdrawn pharmaceutical in it that nobody told you about and nobody measured the dose of.

If a product is natural, it does not need to hide a drug inside it. If it needs to hide a drug inside it, it was never natural.

The others, briefly

  • Waist trainers. They reshape you while you wear them and do nothing at all when you take them off. Worn tight for long periods they compress your ribs, your stomach and your lungs. They do not burn fat — fat is not squeezed out of a person.
  • “Detox” anything. You already own two organs that do this full time and they do it better than tea. Your liver and your kidneys are not waiting for assistance from a sachet.
  • Fat-burner injections and drips. Whatever is in the syringe, nobody has told you, and nobody is watching what it does to you afterwards.
  • Slimming coffee. Same story as the capsules, with caffeine on top to make you feel something is happening.

What you are actually drinking

The honest comparison

Ten thousand naira of flat tummy tea buys you a week of going to the toilet. The same money buys a tailor’s tape, a month of eggs and beans, and a pair of shoes you can walk in — and one of those two options is still working next year.

Why the scale moves and nothing changes

This is worth understanding properly, because it is the trick that every one of these products relies on.

A grown adult carries several kilograms of water and a couple more of food moving through the gut at any moment. Push that out with a laxative or a diuretic and the scale drops two kilograms overnight.

Fat does not move like that. Losing half a kilogram of actual fat takes about a week of doing everything right. Anything faster than that is water, and water always comes back.

Which is the real reason this book asks you to measure your waist instead of standing on a scale. A tape measure cannot be fooled by a laxative.

What a real week looks like

Half a kilogram to one kilogram of fat a week. Slow, unglamorous, and permanent. In ten days that is one to two kilograms. Over the ninety days this book actually runs for, it is seven to thirteen kilograms and seven to twelve centimetres off your waist — which is less than the tea promised for the first fortnight, and it is the only one of the two still there in December.

Today’s secret — Liquid sugar does not make you full

Your body has a fullness system and it barely notices anything you drink. Eat 250 calories of rice and you feel it; drink 250 calories of malt and you feel nothing at all — and you eat the same dinner an hour later.

Do the arithmetic on your own week. Count every bottle of malt, soft drink and beer you had in the last seven days, and multiply by 250. Most people find a number between 3,000 and 7,000 — which is roughly one to two whole days of eating, drunk standing up, unnoticed.

Today’s secret — A cup of oil is about 900

Deep frying is the other half. Food does not merely cook in oil, it drinks it. The same piece of chicken grilled and deep fried differs by more than most people’s entire daily deficit.

Chapter five

05

Days 11 to 90 — where your body actually changes

The ten days were not the programme. The ten days were how you switched everything on without being overwhelmed.

Here is the honest arithmetic. Real fat loss runs at about half a kilogram to one kilogram a week. Over ten days that is one to two kilograms — genuinely good, and not something anybody photographs. Over ninety days the same rate is seven to thirteen kilograms, which is a different body.

Nothing changes about what you are doing. The ten switches simply keep running, and this chapter tells you what each stretch of the next eighty days is going to feel like — including the part where it stops working, which it will, around week six.

If you are losing faster than this

More than about a kilogram a week, sustained, is not extra discipline paying off. It is water, or muscle, or something in a capsule that nobody declared. All three come back, and the third one can hurt you. Slow is the mechanism, not a consolation.

The three days that matter: 30, 60 and 90

Do the same four things on each of them. It takes fifteen minutes and it is the whole record.

  • Measure your waist — morning, standing, at the navel, breathing out normally.
  • Take the photograph — same light, same spot, same clothes, front and side.
  • Audit the ten switches honestly. Not “am I trying”. Switch by switch: is this one still running, yes or no?
  • Write all three down in the log at the back, with the date.

The audit is the important one

Almost everybody whose progress has stopped has quietly dropped two or three switches without noticing. Usually Switch 1 (a bottle crept back in), Switch 4 (the oil stopped being measured) or Switch 9 (the bag stopped being packed). You will not remember this. That is exactly why it is written down as a checklist.

Week six, and why it is not failure

Somewhere between week five and week nine, it stops.

The scale sits still for ten days. The waist does not move. You are doing everything you were doing in week two and nothing is happening. This is the single commonest point at which people decide the plan has stopped working and go back to how they were eating.

It has not stopped working. Two ordinary things are happening at once.

  • You are smaller now, so you need less. A body seven kilograms lighter burns measurably less than it did in week one, just carrying itself around. The same plan that created a gap in week two creates a smaller one in week seven.
  • Portions have crept. Not deliberately. The measured spoon of oil becomes a generous spoon. Half the swallow becomes two thirds. Nobody decides to do this and everybody does it.

What to do about the plateau — and what not to

Do: the audit above, honestly. Re-measure the oil with an actual spoon for a week. Add ten minutes to the daily walk. Give it a fortnight.

Do not: cut your food further, skip meals, or add a second workout out of frustration. Eating less on top of a plateau is how bodies learn that food is unreliable, and it is the exact mechanism that puts weight back on afterwards with interest.

The only rule that matters after Day 10

Never miss twice

One bad meal is a meal. One bad day is a day. The damage begins when a bad day is used as proof that you cannot do this, and becomes a bad month. Nobody ever regained twenty kilograms from one owambe. Eat the next meal properly and the week is still yours.

The week you slip

It will happen inside ninety days. When it does, the book asks three things:

  • Do not weigh yourself. Not that week. You will see water and salt and read it as fat, and the number will make a decision the food never would have.
  • Restart with Switch 1, not all ten. Take the bottles out again and eat normally otherwise. One switch is a restart you will actually make; ten is a resolution you will not.
  • Do not add punishment. No skipping meals to make up for it, no double walking. That teaches your body that food is unreliable, which is the pattern that put the weight on in the first place.

Owambe, parties and eating out

Over ninety days you will go to several. Plan for them instead of pretending you will not.

  • Do not arrive hungry. A boiled egg and a glass of water before you leave. Arriving starving at a party with jollof and small chops is not a test of character, it is a losing position.
  • Protein first — meat, fish, moi moi — then vegetables, then a small portion of rice last. Switch 2 works at a party exactly as it works at home.
  • Hold water, not a bottle. Most of the damage at a Nigerian party is drunk, not eaten. Two malts and a beer is most of a day, standing up, talking.
  • One plate, sitting down. Not three passes standing at a table.
  • Eat normally the next morning. Not a punishment fast. That swing is what ends plans.
The people who keep it off are not the ones who never slipped. They are the ones who never turned a slip into a verdict.

Chapter six

06

The home workout plan

No gym. No equipment. No going anywhere. Four moves, a wall and a chair.

This is not here to burn the food off — Chapter 4 was honest about that. It is here to protect your muscle while the fat comes off, because muscle is what keeps your metabolism up and what stops the weight coming back with interest.

Wall squat

Back flat against a wall, feet a step forward. Slide down until your knees are bent to about a right angle. Hold. Breathe normally — do not hold your breath.

Chair stand

Sit at the front of a chair, arms crossed. Stand up without using your hands, then lower yourself back down slowly — slower going down than coming up.

Wall press-up

Hands flat on a wall at shoulder height and width. Keep your body in one straight line, lower your chest towards the wall, push back.

Marching on the spot

Lift each knee towards hip height, arms swinging. Two minutes. This is your warm-up and it is also a full workout on a day you cannot leave the house.

Plus the walking, which matters more

Thirty minutes most days, and ten minutes straight after your biggest meal. If you only do one kind of movement, do the walking. If you can do two, keep the ten minutes after eating.

If your knees or your back hurt

  • Knees. Do not go as low in the wall squat — a quarter of the way down still works. Skip the chair stands and hold the wall squat longer instead. Never work through sharp pain.
  • Back. Keep your back flat against the wall through the whole squat. If the chair stand hurts, put a cushion on the chair to raise you.
  • Both. Every kilogram you lose takes roughly four kilograms of load off each knee with every step you take. The weight loss is the joint treatment.

Stop and see a doctor if

You get chest pain or tightness, severe breathlessness, dizziness or an irregular heartbeat while moving. None of those is normal, and none of them is something to push through.

Chapter seven

07

Measuring it properly

More plans have been destroyed by a bathroom scale than by any food.

Why the scale lies, especially in week one

A grown adult carries several kilograms of water, and it moves. Salt holds it, heat loses it, and your body holds extra water for a few days after you start using your muscles more than usual — which is exactly when you start a plan.

So it is entirely normal to do everything right for a week and see the scale sit still or go up. Meanwhile your waist is falling.

Fat is slow and it is honest. Half a kilogram to one kilogram a week is what real fat loss looks like. Anything faster is water, and water always comes back.

The tape, and exactly where to put it

  • First thing in the morning, before eating or drinking, after the toilet.
  • Standing up straight, not sitting, not lying down.
  • At the navel — not at the narrowest point, not where your trousers sit. The navel, every time.
  • Breathe out normally and then measure. Not sucked in, not pushed out.
  • Once a week, same day. Not daily. Daily measurement gives you noise and worry.

The numbers to aim below

Under 80cm for a woman, under 94cm for a man. If you are a long way above those, do not aim at them yet — aim at five centimetres less than today, and then aim again.

The photograph nobody wants to take

Take one today. Same light, same spot, same clothes, front and side.

You will hate it. Take it anyway and do not look at it again for six weeks — and then take the second one. The mirror lies to you slowly, because you see yourself every day and change is invisible at that speed. Two photographs six weeks apart are the only honest mirror there is.

Chapter eight

08

When weight needs a doctor, not a plan

Sometimes it is not your discipline. Sometimes it is a hormone, and no amount of trying fixes a hormone.

This chapter exists because a book that never says this is quietly blaming you for something that is not your fault.

Go and ask for a test if

  • Weight has climbed steadily for no reason you can name, with no real change in how you eat.
  • You are tired all the time, cold when others are not, with dry skin, thinning hair or constipation. That combination is worth a thyroid test.
  • Your periods are irregular or absent, with weight around the middle, acne or unusual hair growth. That combination is worth asking about PCOS.
  • You started a new medicine and the weight followed. Several common ones do this — some for mental health, steroids, some for diabetes, some contraceptives. Do not stop any of them. Tell the doctor who prescribed it and ask whether there is an alternative.
  • You snore heavily and wake unrefreshed. Sleep apnoea makes weight loss much harder and it is treatable.
  • You are losing weight without trying. That is not good news and it needs looking at quickly.

What to ask for, plainly

“Please, can I have a thyroid test, a fasting blood sugar and a full blood count?” Those three are ordinary, widely available, and between them they catch most of what this chapter is about. Take the answers away written down.

And one thing not to do

Do not buy weight-loss injections or capsules from anybody who is not a doctor who has examined you. There are real, effective, prescribed weight-loss medicines now — and there is a very large market selling fakes, wrong doses and withdrawn drugs beside them. If it is real medicine, it comes with a real consultation.
