Before anything else

5 things you have been told about losing weight that are costing you money

You’ve probably heard all five of these from somebody, and a few of them may have cost you money. They’re also a big part of why the last attempt didn’t last.

Myth 1

“Carbohydrate is the enemy. I must stop eating swallow.”

The truth

Very few people can keep that up, and you don’t need to.

Every plan that tells a Nigerian to stop eating eba, amala, rice and yam lasts about three weeks and then falls apart, because it feels like punishment and nobody can live like that for long. This book doesn’t take any food away from you. It changes the order you eat things in, and the size of one thing on your plate.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 2.

Myth 2

“Flat tummy tea is natural, so it’s safe.”

The truth

Most of it is a laxative, and some has been found to contain a drug that was banned for causing strokes.

What you lose on a laxative is water and whatever was in your bowel, and it all comes back the day you stop, because no fat left your body. “Natural” is only a word printed on the pack. Slimming products sold in this part of the world have been caught more than once with sibutramine inside, a drug that was taken off the market around the world in 2010 after it caused heart attacks and strokes.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

Myth 3

“I’m not losing weight, so nothing is working.”

The truth

The scale is the least useful number in this book.

In the first week your body lets go of some water and then holds some back, so the scale can stay the same while your waist is getting smaller. Use a tape measure. If your waist has come down by two centimetres, you’re making real progress, even if the scale hasn’t moved at all.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 7.

Myth 4

“I must do heavy exercise or it won’t count.”

The truth

Most of the weight comes off in the kitchen. Walking is what helps keep it off.

An hour of hard exercise burns about the same as one bottle of malt and a sausage roll. So do keep moving, because it protects your muscle and helps stop the weight from coming back. Just don’t expect the gym to make up for what’s on your plate.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 6.

Myth 5

“Mine is hereditary. It’s my body, nothing works.”

The truth

Some of it may run in the family, and some of it could be a thyroid nobody has checked.

Families do share body shapes. They also share one kitchen, one way of cooking and one idea of what a full plate should look like. If your weight keeps climbing and you can’t work out why, get your thyroid checked before you blame yourself. It’s a cheap test, and willpower can’t fix a hormone problem.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 8.

If you believed even one of these

Don’t feel bad about it. It means the plans you tried before were set up to fail. Keep reading.

About this book

If you’re like most people who pick up this book, you’ve tried to lose weight before.

Maybe you started on a Monday and bought the slimming tea. Maybe you skipped dinner for two weeks and felt proud of yourself, until Saturday came and you ate everything in the house. Maybe you lost seven kilos once and then put back nine.

Please don’t blame yourself for any of that. Every one of those plans asked you to stop eating the food you grew up on, and very few people anywhere can keep that up for long.

What this book is

This book gives you ten switches, one a day for ten days. Each switch is an exchange. You put one thing down and pick something else up in its place, and what you pick up is food you already eat and already enjoy.

That’s what the 10X stands for: ten exchanges. You won’t find pills in here, or tea, or imported powder, or any machine to buy.

After the first ten days, you keep the switches going for ninety days. The ten days let you switch everything on one step at a time, so it never feels like too much at once. Over the ninety days is when you’ll really see your body change. Chapter 5 takes you through every week, including week six, which is where a lot of people give up.

What to expect

By Day 10: two to four centimetres off your waist, and a lot less bloating. You won’t be a new size yet, but you’ll know it’s working.

By Day 90: somewhere between seven and thirteen kilograms lighter, and seven to twelve centimetres off your waist. This is usually when people around you start asking what you’ve been doing.

If anything promises you more than that in less time, it’s selling you water weight or a banned drug. Chapter 3 shows you how to tell the difference.

How to use it

  • Read it tonight and start tomorrow morning. Don’t wait for Monday. We all know how the Monday plan usually ends.
  • Keep every switch you turn on. Day 4 goes on top of Day 3, so by Day 10 you have all ten going at once, and they stay on for the eighty days after that.
  • Measure your waist on Day 1, Day 10 and then every Monday. Use a tape measure rather than a scale. Chapter 7 explains why.
  • You will slip at some point, and that’s normal. There’s a page in Chapter 5 for that day, and it won’t make you feel guilty.

Chapter one

01

What really changed on our plates

Most diet books sold in Nigeria start by telling you that the food your mother cooked is the problem. I don’t agree with that at all.

Our grandparents ate eba, rice, amala, tuwo, yam, plantain and beans nearly every day of their lives, and most of them never carried the kind of weight many of us carry today.

What changed is the way we eat that food, and four things in particular.

1. The swallow got bigger and the soup got smaller

Take a good look at the next plate you dish. For most of us it’s three quarters amala or rice, a small amount of soup at the side and one small piece of meat. That’s a plate of starch with a little bit of everything else.

The same food, turned around

2. The drinks

This is the biggest one, and hardly anybody counts it. A bottle of Malta Guinness has close to ten teaspoons of sugar in it, and because it’s a drink, your body doesn’t treat it as food. You finish 250 calories in a minute and a half and you’re still hungry afterwards.

3. More oil in the pot

Palm oil itself is fine. The trouble is how much of it goes in. A lot of us now pour oil straight from the bottle into the pot, and we deep fry far more often than people used to.

4. We stopped walking

Our parents walked to the market, to school and to the farm. Today most of us take a keke or okada to the junction, ride a danfo to work, and then sit in a chair for nine hours.

The good news

All four of these are habits, and habits can be changed. You can fix every one of them without giving up Nigerian food, buying anything imported or paying for a gym.

Why the weight came back last time

Most crash diets work by making you suffer, and nobody can suffer forever.

When you cut your food in half, the weight does come off for a while. But your body can’t tell the difference between a diet and a time of hunger, so it slows down a little to protect you. Meanwhile you’re tired of being hungry all the time, and one day you give up. The weight comes back, often with a bit extra.

That’s why every switch in this book is meant to be permanent. None of them is painful, so there’s nothing to run away from after a few weeks.

Use a tape measure, not a scale

Buy a tailor’s tape measure. Any tailor or market stall will sell you one for very little.

The number on the scale includes water, food still sitting in your stomach, and muscle. It can go up or down by two kilos in a single day for reasons that have nothing to do with fat, and that’s enough to discourage anybody.

Your waist tells you about the fat that matters most, the fat packed around your organs. That’s the kind linked to diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease.

Where to put the tape

This is an illustration, to help you picture the change. Twelve centimetres is hard to imagine until you see it, and it’s roughly what ninety days of all ten switches can do for somebody who starts at 104.

The numbers to aim for

For a woman, a waist under 80cm. For a man, under 94cm. Measure at the navel, standing up, first thing in the morning, and breathe out normally. Don’t hold your stomach in.

The two that do half the work

If you only ever did two of these, do Switch 1 and Switch 3.

The bottle switch, because the sugar in our drinks is probably the biggest thing most of us never count. And the size switch, because cutting your amala in half takes away about a quarter of the meal without taking any food off the table.

Those two alone will do more for you than any slimming tea, and they cost you nothing.

Why the order you eat in makes a difference

Switch 2 is the one people tend to laugh at, but it’s one of the most useful things in this book.

When you eat a big plate of white rice on an empty stomach, your blood sugar shoots up. Your body then releases a lot of insulin to bring it back down, and one of insulin’s other jobs is storing fat. If you eat the same rice after the fish and vegetables, your sugar rises much more gently, because the protein and fibre slow down how fast food leaves your stomach.

So it’s the same plate and the same amount of food, and the only thing you change is the order.

Switch 2 in three steps

You’ll notice something else too. By the time you get to the swallow you’re already partly full, so you end up leaving some of it on the plate without even trying.

Try it at your next meal

Don’t change what you’re eating today. Just eat it in this order: a glass of water first, then the meat and vegetables, and the swallow last. Then see how much swallow is left on your plate at the end.

Chapter three

03

Flat tummy tea, slimming pills and the waist trainer

There’s a good chance you’ve bought at least one of these, and you’re not alone. They’re usually sold by slim, fine people on Instagram who look exactly the way you’d like to look.

You paid good money for them, so you deserve to know what was inside.

What is inside the tea

Read the label on almost any flat tummy tea and you’ll find the same kind of ingredients: senna, cascara, aloe, and sometimes a herb that makes you pass urine more. All of these are laxatives or water pills.

So you drink it, you run to the toilet a few times, you feel lighter, and the next morning the scale shows a kilo less. It feels like it’s working.

All you lost was water and whatever was in your bowel. Your fat is still where it was. Once you stop drinking the tea, the weight comes back within two days or so, which is why the seller keeps telling you to buy another pack.

It can also hurt you

If you take laxatives for a long time, your bowel can get lazy and stop working properly without them. They also wash potassium out of your body, and your heart needs potassium to keep a steady beat. Some people end up in hospital because of this, and it never crosses their mind that the herbal tea was the cause.

The drug that was banned worldwide, and where it turned up

In 2010, a weight-loss drug called sibutramine was taken off the market in Europe, America and other countries. It did help people lose weight, but it also pushed up blood pressure and heart rate, and a large study found it was causing heart attacks and strokes.

Since then, sibutramine has been found again and again inside slimming capsules and teas sold as “100% herbal”, in West Africa, in Asia, online and in open markets. You won’t see it written anywhere on the pack, even though the whole selling point of these products is that they’re natural.

So if a slimming capsule seems to work surprisingly well, it may be because there’s a banned drug inside that nobody told you about, in a dose nobody measured.

Before you swallow any slimming product

Look for a full list of ingredients and a NAFDAC number on the pack. If either one is missing, leave it on the shelf, no matter who is selling it or how many before-and-after pictures they post.

The other things they’ll try to sell you

  • Waist trainers. They hold your stomach in while you’re wearing them, and as soon as you take them off you’re back where you started. Worn tight for hours, they press on your ribs, stomach and lungs, and they don’t burn any fat.
  • “Detox” drinks. Your liver and kidneys already clean your body every single day, and they do it far better than any tea or sachet.
  • Slimming injections and drips. You have no way of knowing what’s in the syringe, and once you leave the place, nobody is checking what it does to you.
  • Slimming coffee. Same story as the capsules, with extra caffeine so you feel like something is happening.

What is really in that bottle

A better way to spend ten thousand naira

Ten thousand naira of flat tummy tea gets you a week of running to the toilet. The same money can buy you a tape measure, a month of eggs and beans, and a good pair of walking shoes, and those will still be helping you next year.

Why the scale moves when your fat doesn’t

It’s worth understanding this, because every one of these products depends on it.

At any moment, a grown adult is carrying several kilos of water, plus a couple more kilos of food making its way through the gut. Push that out with a laxative or a water pill and the scale can drop two kilos overnight.

Fat comes off much more slowly than that. Losing half a kilo of real fat takes about a week of doing everything right. If the scale drops faster than that, most of what you lost is water, and water always comes back.

This is why the book keeps asking you to measure your waist instead. A tape measure shows you the real change.

What real progress looks like

Real fat loss is about half a kilo to one kilo a week. In ten days that’s one to two kilos, and over the full ninety days it adds up to seven to thirteen kilograms and seven to twelve centimetres off your waist. The tea might show a bigger drop in the first two weeks, but that’s water, and it comes back as soon as you stop.

Palm oil is good food. The only problem is how much of it we use, and pouring straight from the bottle makes it very easy to use far more than you think.

Days 11 to 90: when your body really changes

The first ten days were for switching everything on. The real change happens over the next eighty.

Real fat loss happens at about half a kilo to one kilo a week. Over ten days that’s one to two kilos, which is a good start, though most people won’t notice it on you yet. Over ninety days the same pace adds up to seven to thirteen kilograms, and that’s a change everybody can see.

You don’t need to do anything new. You keep the ten switches going, and this chapter tells you what each part of the next eighty days will feel like, including the slow patch that usually comes around week six.

If you’re losing faster than this

If you keep losing more than about a kilo a week, week after week, it’s usually water, muscle, or something in a capsule that wasn’t on the label. That kind of weight tends to come back, and the capsule can hurt you. Slow and steady is what lasts.

Check in on Day 30, 60 and 90

On each of these days, do the same four things. It takes about fifteen minutes.

  • Measure your waist in the morning, standing, at the navel, breathing out normally.
  • Take a photo in the same spot, with the same light and the same clothes, from the front and the side.
  • Check your ten switches one by one. For each one, ask yourself: am I still doing this, yes or no?
  • Write it all down in the log at the back of the book, with the date.

The switch check matters most

When progress stops, it’s very often because two or three switches have slipped without you noticing. Usually it’s Switch 1 (a Malta Guinness here and there), Switch 4 (you stopped measuring the oil) or Switch 9 (you stopped packing your bag). It’s easy to miss, which is why it helps to go through them one at a time.

The week six slowdown, and what to do about it

Somewhere between week five and week nine, things usually slow down.

The scale stays the same for ten days and your waist doesn’t move, even though you’re doing everything you did in week two. This is when a lot of people decide the plan has stopped working and go back to their old way of eating.

The plan is still working. Two normal things are happening at the same time.

  • You’re smaller now, so your body needs less. A body that is seven kilos lighter uses less energy just moving around, so the same food makes a smaller difference than it did at the start.
  • Your portions have slowly grown. The measured spoon of oil becomes a generous spoon, and half the swallow becomes two thirds. Nobody plans it, but almost everybody does it.

What to do when things slow down

Do: go through the switch check above. Measure your oil with a real spoon again for a week, add ten minutes to your daily walk, and give it two weeks.

Don’t: cut your food even more, skip meals or add extra workouts because you’re frustrated. Starving yourself during a slow patch is what makes the weight come back later, often with extra.

One rule for after Day 10

Never miss twice

One bad meal or one bad day won’t undo your progress. The trouble starts when a bad day makes you feel you’ve failed, and it turns into a bad month. Nobody ever put back twenty kilos from one owambe. Eat your next meal properly and carry on.

When you slip

It will probably happen at some point in the ninety days. When it does, do three things:

  • Stay off the scale that week. After a heavy day it will show extra water and salt, and it can make you feel much worse than you should.
  • Start again with Switch 1. Take the drinks out of the fridge again and eat normally otherwise. Starting with one switch is much easier than trying to fix everything in a day.
  • Don’t punish yourself. No skipping meals to make up for it and no double walking. That only starts the same starve-and-binge cycle that put the weight on in the first place.

Owambe, parties and eating out

In ninety days you’ll surely go to a few parties, so plan for them.

  • Don’t arrive hungry. Eat a boiled egg and drink a glass of water before you leave home. If you get there starving, the jollof and small chops will win.
  • Eat your protein first, the meat, fish or moi moi, then any vegetables, and a small portion of rice last. Switch 2 works at a party just like it does at home.
  • Keep a bottle of water in your hand. At most parties the drinks do more damage than the food. Two malts and a beer can add up to about a third of a day’s food while you’re just standing and chatting.
  • Take one plate and sit down to eat it, instead of going back to the food table again and again.
  • Eat normally the next morning. Don’t starve yourself to make up for the party.
Everybody slips. The people who keep the weight off are the ones who get back on track the next day.

The home workout plan

You don’t need a gym or any equipment for this. All you need is a wall and a chair.

These four moves are there to protect your muscle while the fat comes off. Muscle helps your body keep burning energy, and it helps stop the weight from coming back.

Wall squat

Stand with your back flat against a wall and your feet a step forward. Slide down until your knees are bent at about a right angle, and hold it. Keep breathing normally.

Chair stand

Sit near the front of a chair with your arms crossed. Stand up without using your hands, then lower yourself back down slowly, taking longer to sit than to stand.

Wall press-up

Put your hands flat on a wall at shoulder height and width. Keep your body straight, lower your chest towards the wall, then push back.

Marching on the spot

Lift each knee up towards hip height and swing your arms, for two minutes. It’s your warm-up, and on a rainy day when you can’t go out, it can be your whole workout.

Chapter seven

07

Measuring it properly

A lot of people give up on a good plan because of what the bathroom scale told them.

Why the scale can mislead you in the first week

Your body carries several kilos of water, and the amount keeps changing. Salty food makes you hold more, hot weather makes you lose some, and when you start exercising, your muscles hold on to extra water for a few days.

So it’s completely normal to do everything right for a week and see the scale stay the same, or even go up, while your waist is getting smaller.

Fat comes off slowly. Half a kilo to one kilo a week is what real fat loss looks like, and anything faster is usually water.

The tape, and where to put it

  • First thing in the morning, after using the toilet and before you eat or drink anything.
  • Standing up straight.
  • At your navel, every time, even if your trousers usually sit higher or lower.
  • Breathe out normally, then measure. Don’t pull your stomach in.
  • Once a week, on the same day. Measuring every day will only worry you, because small numbers go up and down.

The numbers to aim for

Under 80cm for a woman and under 94cm for a man. If you’re far above that right now, don’t worry about it yet. Aim for five centimetres less than today, and when you get there, aim for five more.

The photograph nobody wants to take

Take one today, from the front and from the side, in a spot you can use again with the same light and the same clothes.

You probably won’t like it. Take it anyway, put it away for six weeks, and then take another one in the same spot. You see yourself in the mirror every day, so you won’t notice the change while it’s happening slowly. Two photos six weeks apart will show you what the mirror can’t.

Chapter eight

08

When to see a doctor about your weight

Sometimes weight gain has a medical cause, and trying harder won’t fix it.

If that’s the case for you, it isn’t your fault, and a doctor can help.

Go and ask for a test if

  • Your weight keeps going up and you haven’t changed the way you eat.
  • You’re tired all the time, you feel cold when others don’t, and you have dry skin, thinning hair or constipation. Ask for a thyroid test.
  • Your periods are irregular or have stopped, and you have weight around your middle, pimples, or hair growing where it didn’t before. Ask your doctor about PCOS.
  • You started a new medicine and then gained weight. Some medicines for mental health, steroids, some diabetes medicines and some family planning methods can do this. Don’t stop any of them on your own. Tell the doctor who gave it to you and ask if there’s another option.
  • You snore loudly and wake up tired. A condition called sleep apnoea makes it harder to lose weight, and it can be treated.
  • You’re losing weight without trying. See a doctor soon, because this needs checking.

What to ask for

“Please, can I have a thyroid test, a fasting blood sugar test and a full blood count?” These are common tests that most hospitals and labs can do, and together they cover most of what this chapter talks about. Ask for your results in writing.

One thing to avoid

Don’t buy weight-loss injections or capsules from anyone except a doctor who has examined you. There are real weight-loss medicines available now, but there are also many fakes, wrong doses and banned drugs being sold on social media and in the market. Real medicine comes with a proper check-up.
