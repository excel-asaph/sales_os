Before anything else

5 things you have probably been told about BP that are not true

Every one of these is common, every one of them sounds sensible, and every one of them is costing somebody their kidneys or their speech right now.

Myth 1

“I feel fine, so my BP is fine.”

The truth

Feeling fine is exactly how it works.

High blood pressure has no feeling. None. It is not a headache, it is not dizziness, it is not heat in the body. Most people with dangerously high pressure feel completely normal on the morning it finally does its damage. The only way to know is to measure it, and the only reason anybody thinks otherwise is that nobody ever told them.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 1.

Myth 2

“I will take the drug until my BP comes down, then stop.”

The truth

The moment you stop, it goes back up — and sometimes higher than before.

This is the single most expensive mistake in this book. Your reading is low because of the tablet, not instead of it. Stopping does not prove you are cured; it proves the tablet was working. And stopping some BP drugs suddenly can send pressure up harder than it was at the start.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 7.

Myth 3

“BP drugs spoil kidney.”

The truth

Uncontrolled pressure is the thing that spoils kidneys. The tablet is what protects them.

This one has cost more Nigerian lives than almost any other sentence. It comes from something real — a doctor checks kidney function after starting certain drugs, and people assume the test means damage. It means the opposite: they are watching. Meanwhile high pressure is the second biggest cause of kidney failure in this country.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 7.

Myth 4

“I have reduced my salt. I no longer add salt at the table.”

The truth

Table salt was never where most of it was coming from.

Two seasoning cubes carry almost the entire sodium a grown adult should have in a whole day — before any salt, stockfish, crayfish, ponmo or canned fish enters the pot. This is the chapter that changes people’s readings, and it is the one nobody has ever explained to them.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

Myth 5

“Somebody’s herbal mixture brought their BP down.”

The truth

Some of those mixtures raise pressure, and a few contain the drug they are pretending to replace.

Ask that person one question: can I see the reading before, and the reading after, from the same machine? Then wait. And understand what you are risking — unlabelled preparations have been found containing steroids and licorice, both of which push blood pressure up, in a person who has stopped their real tablet to take them.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

About this book

Somebody took your arm, pumped a cuff, looked at a machine and said a number. Then they said “your BP is high”, wrote something, and called the next person.

That was probably the whole conversation. Nobody explained what the two numbers mean. Nobody told you what is actually happening inside you, or how fast, or what would change it. You were handed a prescription and sent home to guess.

So let us do the part they skipped.

What this book is

It is written for a Nigerian adult who has just been told their pressure is high, or who has known for years and has never had it properly under control. It is written in ordinary language. Every food in it is sold in your market, at the usual price. Nothing in it is imported, exotic, or expensive.

What it will also do is show you exactly where your pressure is coming from, including the two places nobody has looked, and give you ten days of small, specific, cheap things that bring it down.

One number worth holding on to

Roughly one in three Nigerian adults has high blood pressure, and the large majority of them do not know it. Of those who do know, most are not controlled. You are already ahead of almost everybody by simply knowing.

You cannot feel blood pressure. That is not a reason to relax. It is the entire reason this book exists.

Chapter one

01

The two numbers, and what they are doing

140 over 90. Two numbers, a slash, and nobody explained either of them.

Here they are, in plain words.

The top number is the push against the walls of your blood vessels at the moment your heart squeezes. Doctors call it systolic. Think of it as the hardest shove.

The bottom number is the push that is still there while the heart is resting between beats. Doctors call it diastolic. Think of it as the pressure that never lets go.

That second one matters more than people realise. A hose under constant pressure does not burst on the day you turn the tap. It wears, and thins, and one ordinary morning it gives way.

What counts as high

These are the levels the World Health Organization uses, and they are the ones most clinics in this country work from.

ReadingWhat it is calledWhat it means for you
Under 120 / 80NormalKeep it there. Check once a year.
120–139 / 80–89RaisedNot yet hypertension. This is the stage where food and walking alone can still turn it around.
140/90 or aboveHypertensionConfirmed on two different days, this is a diagnosis. Treatment is recommended.
180/120 or aboveCrisisGo to hospital the same day. Do not wait for morning.

Three things about that table

One high reading is not a diagnosis. Pressure moves. It rises when you have rushed, argued, climbed stairs, drunk coffee, or sat in a clinic corridor worrying. A diagnosis needs readings on two separate days.

Either number counts. 138 over 96 is high blood pressure. People look at the top number only and relax when they should not.

American guidelines call 130/80 high. If somebody quotes you a different number, that is where it comes from. Your doctor sets which one applies to you.

What it is doing while you feel well

This is the part that never gets said in the consulting room, and it is the only part that matters.

High pressure does not hurt. It does not make you dizzy. It does not make your head hot. Those things happen to people with normal pressure every day. What high pressure does is wear out four organs quietly, over years, while you feel completely like yourself.

  • Your brain. The small vessels deep inside it are the most delicate in the body. Pressure damages them first. That is a stroke, and it is why stroke is what this book keeps coming back to.
  • Your heart. A pump pushing against resistance thickens, the way any muscle does. A thickened heart is a stiff heart, and a stiff heart eventually fails.
  • Your kidneys. They are made almost entirely of tiny blood vessels. High pressure is the second biggest cause of kidney failure in Nigeria. Dialysis in this country costs more per year than most families earn.
  • Your eyes. The only place a doctor can look directly at your blood vessels without cutting you open. The damage there is a preview of the damage everywhere else.

None of those four give a warning

There is no early symptom. There is no ache that tells you the kidneys are going. By the time something announces itself, a great deal has already happened — and almost none of it needed to.

This is why a reading matters more than how you feel. Your body has no way of telling you this one.

Feeling well is not evidence. With this condition, feeling well is simply what it feels like until the day it does not.

Chapter two

02

The stroke conversation

Everybody in this country knows somebody it happened to. Almost nobody knows what it actually is.

A stroke is a blood vessel in the brain either blocking or bursting. When that happens, the part of the brain it was feeding starts dying within minutes. What that person loses — speech, an arm, a side of the face, the ability to swallow — depends entirely on which part.

High blood pressure is the single biggest cause. Not stress on its own. Not spiritual attack. Not cold water. Pressure, working quietly on those vessels for years.

The signs that mean go now

If any one of these appears, suddenly, it is an emergency. Not a wait-till-morning. Not a rub-with-ointment. The treatment that reverses a stroke only works in the first few hours.

  • Face. One side droops, or the smile is crooked.
  • Arm. One arm is weak, or drifts down when both are raised.
  • Speech. Words are slurred, wrong, or will not come.
  • Time. Any of those three — go to hospital immediately.

Also: sudden severe headache unlike any before, sudden loss of vision in one eye, sudden loss of balance. Same answer. Go.

What nobody says about stroke

It is the most preventable serious thing that can happen to you.

Not a bit preventable. Bringing a high pressure down into a normal range removes most of the risk. Not by surgery, not by anything exotic — by a tablet taken daily, less salt, and a walk.

Think about what that means. The thing everybody in your family is quietly afraid of is, for you, largely a decision. You are holding the lever.

The honest summary of this chapter

The man who has a stroke at 54 and the man who does not are very often the same man, with the same pressure, separated only by whether anybody was measuring it and whether he took the tablet.

Why this book leans on the fear once, and then stops

Because fear does not keep anybody taking a tablet for twenty years. It works for about a week.

What keeps people going is that the plan is small, the food is food they already like, and the numbers on their own machine start moving where they can see them. That is the rest of this book. This was the only chapter that needed to frighten you, and it is finished now.

Chapter three

03

The salt you cannot see

You have already been told to reduce salt. You probably did. You stopped putting the salt shaker on the table, and your pressure did not move, and you quietly decided the advice was rubbish.

The advice was not rubbish. It was just incomplete in a way that made it useless.

Table salt was never where most of your sodium was coming from. In this country it comes out of a small silver wrapper, and it goes into the pot before anybody tastes anything.

The arithmetic nobody has shown you

An adult should have no more than 2,000mg of sodium a day. That is the World Health Organization figure, and it is not a suggestion for sick people — it is the ceiling for everybody.

Now here is what is in the wrapper. Testing of the leading seasoning cubes sold in Nigerian markets found sodium at an average of 22.8 grams per 100 grams of cube. A standard cube weighs about four grams.

What goes in the potSodiumShare of your whole day
1 seasoning cubeabout 910mg46%
2 seasoning cubesabout 1,820mg91%
3 seasoning cubesabout 2,730mg137% — over the limit
½ teaspoon of salt, added afterabout 1,150mg58%

Read the second row again. Two cubes is almost your entire day, and that is before one grain of salt, one piece of stockfish, one spoon of crayfish or one cube of ponmo has gone anywhere near the pot.

Most Nigerian homes use two to four cubes in a single pot of stew, and then that stew is eaten twice.

This is why your tablet looks like it is not working

People come back after three months, still at 160, and assume the drug is weak or that their case is spiritual. Very often the drug is fine. It is simply being asked to pull against three cubes a day, every day, and no tablet is strong enough for that.

The tablet and the cube are fighting each other in your body. One of them has to go, and it is not going to be the tablet.

The rest of the hidden list

Cubes are the biggest one, but they are not alone. Everything here is salty before you salt it.

  • Stockfish and dried fish. Preserved with salt — that is what preserving means. Soak it, throw the first water away, and use less of it.
  • Crayfish. Dried and salted. A good flavour, and a hidden load. Use a teaspoon, not a handful.
  • Ponmo and processed meat. Corned beef, sausage, sardine in tin, anything that came out of a tin at all.
  • Bread. Nobody thinks of bread as salty. Three slices carries more sodium than most people guess, and it arrives every single morning.
  • “Chinese” and other flavour powders. Same family as the cube, same problem.
  • Instant noodles. The sachet is the whole problem. Use half, or none.
  • Baking soda in beans. Sodium is in the name. Cook the beans longer instead.

The one-week experiment

Do not change anything else. For seven days, take the cubes out and use the flavour list on the next page instead. Measure your BP on the first morning and the eighth. Most people see the top number fall by four to eight points from this alone. Write both numbers down — seeing it yourself is what makes it stick.

How to cook with real flavour and almost no salt

Here is the fear, and it is a fair one: my food will taste like nothing, and my family will complain.

They will, for about five days. Your tongue resets faster than you expect — after a week or two of less salt, the old way starts tasting harsh. In the meantime, you replace salt with the things that were always doing the real work anyway.

Use these freely

  • Onion, plenty, browned slowly
  • Garlic and ginger, fresh, pounded
  • Fresh pepper — rodo, tatashe, shombo
  • Uziza, scent leaf, curry leaf, thyme
  • Locust bean (iru, dawadawa) — rinse it first
  • Smoked fish for aroma, not salted fish
  • Lime or lemon at the end
  • Tomato cooked down properly

Small quantities only

  • Crayfish — 1 teaspoon
  • Stockfish — soaked, first water thrown
  • Ordinary salt — a pinch, at the end
  • Palm oil — measured, not poured
  • Bread — 1 to 2 slices

Out of the kitchen

  • Seasoning cubes, all brands
  • Flavour powders and “Chinese”
  • Instant noodle sachets
  • Tinned corned beef and sausage
  • Baking soda in beans
  • Table salt shaker on the table

The trick that does most of the work

Add your pinch of salt at the very end, off the heat. Salt on the surface hits your tongue directly and tastes far saltier than the same pinch boiled into the pot for an hour. You get the taste for a quarter of the sodium.

The chemist trap

This one catches thousands of people, and almost nobody is warned.

The ordinary painkillers sold across every counter in Nigeria — diclofenac, ibuprofen, piroxicam, and the mixed tablets that contain them — raise blood pressure. They also blunt several BP tablets, so you get hit twice. Somebody with a bad back who takes diclofenac daily for a month can undo an entire prescription.

Say this at the chemist, every time

“I am on blood pressure medicine. Is this safe with it?”

Twelve words. Ask it for painkillers, for cold and catarrh medicine, for “blood tonic”, for anything at all. Paracetamol is the usual safer answer for pain, but the point is to ask rather than to guess.

Bitters, alcohol and the mixtures sold for BP

Alcohol raises blood pressure directly, and it is one of the few things that does so within hours. The bitters culture makes this worse, because people do not count bitters as alcohol. It is alcohol, usually strong, usually taken daily, and usually on top of the tablet.

Then there are the mixtures sold specifically for blood pressure. Understand what you are risking:

  • Some raise pressure. Licorice root, which appears in many preparations, is a documented cause of high blood pressure and low potassium.
  • Some contain real drugs, unlabelled. Steroids have been found in unlabelled preparations across this region. Steroids push pressure up.
  • The real danger is what they replace. Almost everybody who starts a mixture stops their tablet. That is the stroke.
Somebody will tell you their uncle drank something and his BP came down completely. Ask to see the reading before and the reading after, from the same machine. Then wait. You will be waiting a long time.

Chapter four

04

The 10-day pressure reset

Ten days. Not ten days to a cure — ten days to a number that is lower than the one you have today, measured on your own machine, by you.

Blood pressure is unusual among serious conditions in that it answers quickly. Take the salt out and the top number starts moving within a week. Stop the alcohol and it moves within hours. Start the four-minute exercise on Day 2 and it shows inside a fortnight. You do not have to believe any of this, because on Day 10 you take the same reading you took on Day 1 and you look at the two of them.

How each day works

  • First thing this morning. One action, done before the day gets away from you.
  • Three meals and what to take between them. Ordinary Nigerian food, from your market, at the usual price.
  • Today’s recipe. Full quantities, full method, no seasoning cube anywhere in this book.
  • Today’s move. Mostly four minutes against a wall. Read Day 2 before you dismiss it.
  • Today’s secret. One thing a day that nobody has told you, and that genuinely moves the number.
  • This morning’s reading. A box to write it in. Fill it every single day.

Before you start, three rules

Do not stop any tablet. Nothing in these ten days replaces your medicine, and stopping it is the one thing that can genuinely hurt you.

Measure at the same time every morning, before food, before coffee, sitting, after five quiet minutes. A reading taken any other way is not comparable and will only confuse you.

If any reading is 180/120 or above, stop the plan and go to hospital that day. That is not a number to manage at home.

You are not being asked to believe this book. You are being asked to write down two numbers, ten days apart, and let them argue it out.

Today’s recipe — Catfish Pepper Soup, no cube
Ingredients
  • Fresh catfish – 2 medium steaks, well washed
  • Onion – ½, sliced
  • Fresh pepper – to taste
  • Ginger – a thumb, pounded
  • Uziza or scent leaf – 1 handful
  • Pepper soup spice – 1 tsp
  • Salt – one pinch, at the very end
  • Water – 2 cups
Preparation

Boil the water with onion, ginger, pepper and spice. Add the fish, cover, simmer 10–12 minutes without stirring hard. Leaves in for the last 2 minutes. Take it off the heat, then add the pinch of salt.

Makes: 2 servings. Keeps 2 days covered in the fridge.

Today’s move

A 10-minute walk, any time today. That is all. You are not training, you are starting.

Today’s secret — Measure both arms — once, today

Take the reading on your left arm, then on your right. Write both down. From tomorrow, use whichever arm read higher, every time.

If the two arms differ by more than about 15 points on the top number, tell your doctor. A real gap between arms can mean a narrowed vessel, and it is something they will want to look at.

And check the cuff. A cuff that is too small for your arm reads falsely high — sometimes by 10 to 20 points. If you have a big arm, buy the large cuff. People have been treated for years on a number that was never real.

Today’s secret — Two cubes is 91% of your whole day

Two cubes is 1,820mg. That is your entire day, before salt, before stockfish, before crayfish, before ponmo.

This single change moves the top number by four to eight points in most people within a week. You will see it yourself on Day 10.

Today’s recipe — Zobo With Ginger, No Sugar
Ingredients
  • Dried zobo (hibiscus) leaves – 2 cups, rinsed
  • Water – 2 litres
  • Ginger – a large thumb, sliced
  • Cloves – 4

Preparation

Boil the water with ginger and cloves. Take it off the heat, pour it over the zobo leaves, cover and leave it 20 minutes. Sieve. Cool, and keep it in the fridge. Drink one to two glasses a day.

Today’s secret — Zobo lowers blood pressure — if you leave the sugar out

This is not a folk claim. Hibiscus has been through more than a dozen randomised trials, and the pooled result is a fall of roughly 7 points on the top number and 4 on the bottom. That is in the same range as a starting dose of a real BP tablet.

The sugar is what kills it. A glass of zobo made the usual way carries more sugar than a soft drink, and sugar works against you. Unsweetened, cold, with ginger — that is the medicine version.

Two honest warnings. It does not replace your tablet, it works alongside it. And because it genuinely does something, tell your doctor you are drinking it daily, especially if you take several medicines.

Today’s recipe — Ewa Agoyin Sauce, Cube-Free
Ingredients
  • Brown or honey beans – 2 cups, cooked soft
  • Dried pepper – 6, soaked and blended
  • Onion – 3 large, sliced very thin
  • Palm oil – 3 tbsp, measured
  • Ginger and garlic – 1 tsp each, pounded

Preparation

Cook the beans very soft with no salt and no soda — just time and water. For the sauce, heat the palm oil and fry the onion low and slow for 15 minutes until it is dark and sweet. Add pepper, ginger and garlic, fry 5 more minutes. Salt off the heat. Serve over the beans.

Today’s move

Today’s secret — Potassium pushes sodium out — and it is already in your market

Sodium and potassium work against each other in the body. Eating more potassium lowers blood pressure on its own, and most Nigerians eat far too little of it while eating far too much sodium.

The best sources here are ordinary: ugu, waterleaf, beans, plantain, sweet potato, pawpaw, orange, coconut water, avocado (ube), tomato. Nothing imported, nothing expensive.

One important exception. If you have been told you have kidney disease, or you take a tablet that holds potassium in, ask your doctor before you push potassium up. For that small group, more is not better.

Why four minutes against a wall beats an hour of anything else

In 2023, researchers pooled 270 trials and 15,827 people to compare every kind of exercise for lowering blood pressure. Isometric work — holding a position rather than moving — came out on top at 8.2 points off the top number, against 4.5 for ordinary aerobic exercise and 4.1 for high-intensity training. The wall squat was the single most effective form tested. It is free, it takes four minutes, and it needs nothing but a wall.

Chapter six

06

Your tablet, honestly explained

This is the most important chapter in the book, and it is the one people skip.

What the tablet does

Different BP tablets work in different ways — some relax the vessels, some take extra water and salt out through the kidney, some slow the heart a little. What they all do is reduce the force pushing against your vessel walls, every hour of every day, including the hours you are asleep and the hours you are angry.

What they do not do is cure anything. Your pressure is low because the tablet is in your body. It is not low instead of the tablet.

Never stop it on your own

This is the sentence this whole book is built around. People stop for ordinary, understandable reasons: the reading looked good, the money ran out, somebody at church said they had been healed, a mixture was recommended.

Stopping does not prove you are cured. It proves the tablet was working. And with some BP medicines, stopping suddenly sends the pressure up harder than it was before you started — which is exactly when strokes happen.

If any of those reasons is about to apply to you, tell your doctor first and let them plan it with you. There are cheaper tablets and there are safe ways to change. Disappearing is not one of them.

“BP drugs spoil kidney”

Let us deal with this properly, because it has cost more Nigerian lives than almost any other sentence.

It comes from something real. When you start certain BP tablets, a good doctor checks your kidney function a few weeks later, and sometimes the number shifts slightly. People hear “kidney test” and conclude the drug is attacking the kidney.

It is the exact opposite. The test is being done because the doctor is protecting the kidney. And the thing actually destroying kidneys in this country is uncontrolled blood pressure — it is the second biggest cause of kidney failure in Nigeria, and dialysis costs more in a year than most families earn.

The tablet is not the threat to your kidney. It is the thing standing between your kidney and the pressure.

Why it is usually for life

Because in most people, high blood pressure is not an event that happens and finishes. It is how their body now regulates itself. Take the tablet away and the regulation goes back to where it was.

That sounds heavy until you compare it with the alternative, which is a stroke at fifty-four. A tablet a day is not a sentence. It is the cheapest insurance available to you.

A small number of people — usually those who were only mildly high, and who lose a lot of weight or take out a great deal of salt — do come off medication under a doctor’s supervision. If that is going to be you, your doctor will tell you. You will not discover it by stopping and hoping.

When money is short

Say it out loud in the consulting room. Doctors cannot help with a problem they do not know about, and this one is extremely common.

  • Ask for the generic. The same drug without the brand name is often a fraction of the price and works identically.
  • Ask for a combination tablet. Two drugs in one pill is frequently cheaper than two pills, and easier to remember.
  • Ask which one matters most if you genuinely cannot afford all of them this month. Get the answer written down.
  • Buy the month, not the week. Buying five tablets at a time costs more per tablet and is how people end up with gaps.

Side effects: what to report, what passes

Report: a dry cough that will not stop, swelling of the ankles, feeling faint on standing, a rash. All of these have straightforward fixes, usually a change of tablet. None of them is a reason to simply stop.

Usually passes: passing more urine in the first week, mild tiredness in the first few days. Give it two weeks before you judge.

Chapter seven

07

Measuring it yourself

The single most useful thing you can buy this year costs about the same as two tanks of fuel.

An upper-arm digital blood pressure machine. Not a wrist one — wrist machines are far less reliable and the position of your hand changes the answer. Upper arm, digital, with a cuff that actually fits you.

The cuff is the part nobody checks

A cuff that is too small for your arm reads falsely high — sometimes by 10 to 20 points. People with big arms have been treated for years on numbers that were never real, and others have been frightened into herbal mixtures by a reading that was simply wrong.

If the cuff does not wrap comfortably with room to spare, buy the large size. Say it in the shop: “I need the large cuff.”

The seven rules for a reading you can trust

  • Empty your bladder first. A full bladder adds about 10 points. This alone explains a lot of frightening morning readings.
  • Sit for five minutes, quietly, before you start. Not thirty seconds. Five minutes.
  • Back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs uncrossed. Crossed legs add points.
  • Arm resting on a table at the level of your heart. An arm hanging down reads high; an arm raised reads low.
  • Cuff on bare skin, not over a sleeve.
  • Do not talk, and do not check your phone. Talking during a reading adds up to 10 points.
  • Take two readings a minute apart and write down the second one. The first is almost always the highest.

White coat

Plenty of people read high in a clinic and normal at home. It is real, it has a name, and it is a good reason to bring your own log to appointments. A week of honest home readings tells your doctor far more than one number taken while you were anxious in a corridor.

The numbers that mean go now

180 over 120, or above

Repeat it once after five minutes of sitting. If it is still there, go to hospital the same day. Not tomorrow, not after church.

Go immediately, whatever the reading says, if any of these appear suddenly: chest pain, severe breathlessness, one side of the face dropping, weakness in one arm, speech that will not come, the worst headache of your life, or sudden loss of vision.

What a good week looks like on paper

Two readings a week, same morning, both numbers written down with the date. After a month you will have eight readings, and eight readings is a pattern. A pattern is something a doctor can actually treat.

One high reading in isolation is noise. Do not panic at it, and do not change anything because of it. Write it down and keep going.

A patient who walks in with four weeks of their own readings gets a different consultation from a patient who walks in with a feeling. Every single time.

Chapter eight

08

Your household

High blood pressure runs in families, and you are the one who found out first. That makes you useful.

Partly it is inherited. Mostly it is that a family shares one kitchen, one salt habit, one way of cooking, and one set of ideas about what food should taste like. Change the kitchen and you change everybody in it.

Who should know their number

  • Your husband or wife. Today. You have a machine now; it takes two minutes.
  • Your brothers and sisters. If it is in you, it is likely in them.
  • Your parents, if they are living. Many older Nigerians have never had a reading taken outside a hospital admission.
  • Everybody in the house over thirty. A reading once a year. That is the whole ask.

The strongest reason to cook one way

A child raised in a low-salt kitchen grows up with a tongue that does not need salt, and a much lower chance of ever sitting where you are sitting. You are not putting your family on a diet. You are ending something.

The conversation with a man who will not go

Almost every Nigerian family has one. He feels fine, he has never been sick a day, hospital is for weak people, and he is not going.

Arguing does not work. Three things sometimes do:

  • Do not ask him to go to hospital. Ask him to sit down for two minutes. You have the machine. Take it to him. A number is harder to dismiss than a lecture.
  • Do not talk about him. Talk about the people who depend on him. That is the argument that lands with a man who thinks he is indestructible.
  • Show him the cube page. Men who will not discuss their health will happily argue about food, and that argument gets the salt out of the pot either way.
You are not the sick one at the table. You are the one who found out in time, in a family where most people find out too late.
