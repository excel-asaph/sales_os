Before anything else

5 things you have probably been told about BP that are not true

All five are common and they all sound sensible, but somewhere in Nigeria right now, one of them is costing somebody their kidneys or their speech.

Myth 1

“I feel fine, so my BP is fine.”

The truth

Most people with high blood pressure feel fine.

High blood pressure has no feeling at all. Headaches, dizziness and heat in the body are not how it shows itself. Most people with dangerously high pressure feel completely normal on the morning it finally does its damage. The only way to know is to measure it, and most people think otherwise only because nobody ever told them.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 1.

Myth 2

“I will take the drug until my BP comes down, then stop.”

The truth

The moment you stop, it goes back up, and sometimes higher than before.

This is the most costly mistake in this book. Your reading is low because you are taking the tablet. If it goes up after you stop, that only shows the tablet was working. And stopping some BP drugs suddenly can send pressure up harder than it was at the start.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 6.

Myth 3

“BP drugs spoil kidney.”

The truth

Uncontrolled pressure is what spoils kidneys, and the tablet protects them.

This one has cost more Nigerian lives than almost any other sentence. It comes from something real. A doctor checks kidney function after starting certain drugs, and people assume the test means damage, when it only means the doctor is keeping watch. Meanwhile high pressure is the second biggest cause of kidney failure in this country.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 6.

Myth 4

“I have reduced my salt. I no longer add salt at the table.”

The truth

Table salt was never where most of it was coming from.

Two seasoning cubes carry almost all the sodium a grown adult should have in a whole day, before any salt, stockfish, crayfish, ponmo or tinned fish goes into the pot. This is the chapter that changes people’s readings, and hardly anybody has ever explained it to them.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

Myth 5

“Somebody’s herbal mixture brought their BP down.”

The truth

Some of those mixtures raise pressure, and a few contain the drug they are pretending to replace.

Ask that person one question: can I see the reading before, and the reading after, from the same machine? Then wait. And know what you are risking. Unlabelled mixtures have been found containing steroids and licorice, which both push blood pressure up, and most people who take them have stopped their real tablet to do it.

Dealt with properly in Chapter 3.

If you recognised even one of them

Don’t worry, you haven’t lost anything yet. Keep reading.

About this book

Somebody took your arm, pumped a cuff, looked at a machine and said a number. Then they said “your BP is high”, wrote something, and called the next person.

That was probably the whole conversation. Nobody explained what the two numbers mean, what is happening inside you, how fast, or what would change it. You were handed a prescription and sent home to guess.

So let me explain the part they skipped.

What this book is

It is written for a Nigerian adult who has just been told their pressure is high, or who has known for years and has never had it properly under control. It is written in ordinary language, and every food in it is sold in your market at the usual price.

It will show you where your pressure is coming from, including two places nobody has probably checked, and give you ten days of small, cheap things to do that bring it down.

One number worth holding on to

Roughly one in three Nigerian adults has high blood pressure, and the large majority of them do not know it. Of those who do know, most don’t have it under control. Just by knowing, you are already ahead of most people.

Chapter one

01

The two numbers, and what they are doing

140 over 90. Two numbers with a slash between them, and nobody explained either of them.

This is what they mean.

The top number is the push against the walls of your blood vessels at the moment your heart squeezes. Doctors call it systolic. Think of it as the hardest shove.

The bottom number is the push that is still there while the heart is resting between beats. Doctors call it diastolic. Think of it as the pressure that never lets go.

That second one matters more than people realise. Think of a garden hose that is always under pressure. It doesn’t burst the day you open the tap. It wears thin over time, and then one ordinary morning it gives way.

What counts as high

These are the levels the World Health Organization uses, and they are the ones most clinics in this country work from.

ReadingWhat it is calledWhat it means for you
Under 120 / 80NormalKeep it there. Check once a year.
120–139 / 80–89RaisedNot yet hypertension. At this stage, food and walking alone can still bring it back down.
140/90 or aboveHypertensionConfirmed on two different days, this is a diagnosis. Treatment is recommended.
180/120 or aboveCrisisGo to hospital the same day. Do not wait for morning.

Three things about that table

One high reading is not enough to diagnose you. Pressure moves. It rises when you have rushed, argued, climbed stairs, drunk coffee, or sat in a clinic corridor worrying. A diagnosis needs readings on two separate days.

Either number counts. 138 over 96 is high blood pressure. People look at the top number only and relax when they should not.

American guidelines call 130/80 high. If somebody quotes you a different number, that is where it comes from. Your doctor sets which one applies to you.

What it is doing while you feel well

This part rarely gets said in the consulting room, and it matters more than anything else in this chapter.

High pressure doesn’t hurt, and it doesn’t make you dizzy or make your head hot. Those things happen to people with normal pressure every day. What high pressure does is wear out four organs slowly, over years, while you feel completely like yourself.

  • Your brain. The small vessels deep inside it are the most delicate in the body. Pressure damages them first. That is a stroke, and it is why stroke is what this book keeps coming back to.
  • Your heart. A pump pushing against resistance thickens, the way any muscle does. A thickened heart is a stiff heart, and a stiff heart eventually fails.
  • Your kidneys. They are made almost entirely of tiny blood vessels. High pressure is the second biggest cause of kidney failure in Nigeria. Dialysis in this country costs more per year than most families earn.
  • Your eyes. This is the only place a doctor can look straight at your blood vessels without cutting you open, and the damage there shows what is happening everywhere else.

None of those four give a warning

There is no early symptom, and no ache to tell you the kidneys are going. By the time you notice something, a lot of damage has already been done, and most of it could have been prevented.

This is why a reading matters more than how you feel. Your body has no way of telling you this one.

With high blood pressure you can feel perfectly well right up until the day something goes wrong. Only the machine can tell you.

Chapter two

02

The stroke conversation

Everybody in this country knows somebody it happened to, but very few people know what a stroke is.

A stroke is a blood vessel in the brain either blocking or bursting. When that happens, the part of the brain it was feeding starts dying within minutes. What the person loses depends on which part: it could be their speech, an arm, one side of the face, or the ability to swallow.

High blood pressure is the biggest cause. People blame stress, spiritual attack or cold water, but it is the pressure, working on those vessels for years.

The signs that mean go now

If any one of these comes on suddenly, it is an emergency. Don’t wait till morning, and don’t rub it with ointment and hope. The treatment that reverses a stroke only works in the first few hours.

  • Face. One side droops, or the smile is crooked.
  • Arm. One arm is weak, or drifts down when both are raised.
  • Speech. Words are slurred, wrong, or will not come.
  • Time. If you see any of those three, go to hospital immediately.

The same goes for a sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had before, sudden loss of vision in one eye, or sudden loss of balance. Go straight away.

What nobody says about stroke

Of all the serious things that can happen to you, stroke is one of the easiest to prevent.

Bringing a high pressure down into the normal range removes most of the risk. You don’t need surgery or anything expensive for that, just a tablet taken daily, less salt, and a walk.

Think about what that means. The thing everybody in your family is afraid of is, for you, mostly in your own hands.

In short

Take two men of 54 with the same pressure. Very often, the one who avoids a stroke is the one who kept measuring it and kept taking his tablet.

Why this book leans on the fear once, and then stops

Fear might keep you going for a week, but it won’t keep you taking a tablet for twenty years.

What keeps people going is a small plan, food they already like, and seeing the numbers on their own machine come down. That is what the rest of this book is about. This was the only chapter meant to frighten you, and it’s finished now.

Chapter three

03

The salt you cannot see

You have already been told to reduce salt, and you probably did. You took the salt shaker off the table, your pressure didn’t move, and you decided the advice was rubbish.

The advice was right, but nobody told you the most important part.

Table salt was never where most of your sodium was coming from. In this country it comes out of a small silver wrapper, and it goes into the pot before anybody tastes anything.

How much salt is in a cube

An adult should have no more than 2,000mg of sodium a day. That is the World Health Organization figure, and it is the limit for everybody, sick or well.

Now look at what is in the wrapper. Testing of the leading seasoning cubes sold in Nigerian markets found sodium at an average of 22.8 grams per 100 grams of cube. A standard cube weighs about four grams.

What goes in the potSodiumShare of your whole day
1 seasoning cubeabout 910mg46%
2 seasoning cubesabout 1,820mg91%
3 seasoning cubesabout 2,730mg137%, over the limit
½ teaspoon of salt, added afterabout 1,150mg58%

Read the second row again. Two cubes is almost your whole day, and that is before any salt, stockfish, crayfish or ponmo has gone anywhere near the pot.

Most Nigerian homes use two to four cubes in a single pot of stew, and then that stew is eaten twice.

This is why your tablet seems not to be working

People come back after three months, still at 160, and assume the drug is weak or that their case is spiritual. Very often the drug is fine. It is being asked to pull against three cubes a day, every day, and no tablet is strong enough for that.

The tablet and the cube are fighting each other in your body. The cube is the one that has to go.

The rest of the hidden list

Cubes are the biggest one, but there are others. Everything on this list is salty before you add any salt.

  • Stockfish and dried fish. These are preserved with salt. Soak them, throw the first water away, and use less.
  • Crayfish. It is dried and salted, so it adds a lot of salt along with the flavour. Use a teaspoon instead of a handful.
  • Ponmo and processed meat. Corned beef, sausage, sardine in tin, anything that came out of a tin at all.
  • Bread. Nobody thinks of agege bread as salty, but three slices carry more sodium than most people guess, and many of us eat it every morning.
  • “Chinese” and other flavour powders. Same family as the cube, same problem.
  • Indomie and other instant noodles. Most of the salt is in the seasoning sachet. Use half of it, or none.
  • Potash (kaun) or baking soda in beans. Both add sodium. Cook the beans longer instead.

The one-week experiment

Don’t change anything else. For seven days, take the cubes out and use the flavour list on the next page instead. Measure your BP on the first morning and the eighth. Most people see the top number fall by four to eight points from this alone. Write both numbers down, because seeing it for yourself is what makes you keep going.

How to cook with real flavour and almost no salt

You are probably worried that my food will taste like nothing, and my family will complain. That’s a fair worry.

They will complain for about five days. Your tongue gets used to it faster than you expect, and after a week or two of less salt, the old way starts to taste too salty. In the meantime, you get your flavour from the things below.

Use these freely

  • Onion, plenty, browned slowly
  • Garlic and ginger, fresh, pounded
  • Fresh pepper: rodo, tatashe, shombo
  • Uziza, scent leaf, curry leaf, thyme
  • Locust bean (iru, dawadawa), rinsed first
  • Smoked fish for aroma, instead of salted fish
  • Lime or lemon at the end
  • Tomato cooked down properly

Small quantities only

  • Crayfish: 1 teaspoon
  • Stockfish: soaked, first water thrown away
  • Ordinary salt: a pinch, at the end
  • Palm oil: measured with a spoon
  • Bread: 1 to 2 slices

Out of the kitchen

  • Seasoning cubes, all brands
  • Flavour powders and “Chinese”
  • Instant noodle sachets
  • Tinned corned beef and sausage
  • Baking soda in beans
  • Table salt shaker on the table

The trick that helps the most

Add your pinch of salt at the very end, off the heat. Salt on the surface hits your tongue directly and tastes far saltier than the same pinch boiled into the pot for an hour. You get the taste for a quarter of the sodium.

The chemist trap

This one catches a lot of people, and almost nobody warns them.

The ordinary painkillers sold at every chemist in Nigeria, diclofenac, ibuprofen, piroxicam and the mixed tablets that contain them, raise blood pressure. They also blunt several BP tablets, so you get hit twice. Somebody with a bad back who takes diclofenac daily for a month can undo an entire prescription.

Say this at the chemist, every time

“I am on blood pressure medicine. Is this safe with it?”

Twelve words. Ask it for painkillers, for cold and catarrh medicine, for “blood tonic”, for anything at all. Paracetamol is usually the safer choice for pain, but always ask instead of guessing.

Bitters, alcohol and the mixtures sold for BP

Alcohol raises blood pressure directly, and it is one of the few things that does so within hours. Bitters make this worse, because people don’t count bitters as alcohol. It is alcohol, it is usually strong, and people tend to take it every day on top of their tablet.

Then there are the mixtures sold for blood pressure. Know what you are risking:

  • Some raise pressure. Licorice root, which appears in many preparations, is a documented cause of high blood pressure and low potassium.
  • Some contain real drugs, unlabelled. Steroids have been found in unlabelled preparations across this region. Steroids push pressure up.
  • The biggest danger is what they replace. Almost everybody who starts a mixture stops their tablet, and that is how strokes happen.
Somebody will tell you their uncle drank something and his BP came down completely. Ask to see the reading before and the reading after, from the same machine, and see how long you wait.

Chapter four

04

The 10-day pressure reset

In ten days you can get your reading lower than it is today, and you will see it for yourself on your own machine.

Blood pressure responds faster than most serious conditions. Take the salt out and the top number starts moving within a week. Stop the alcohol and it moves within hours. Start the four-minute exercise on Day 2 and you will see a difference within two weeks. You don’t have to take my word for it, because on Day 10 you take the same reading you took on Day 1 and compare the two.

How each day works

  • First thing this morning. One action, done before the day gets away from you.
  • Three meals and what to take between them. Ordinary Nigerian food, from your market, at the usual price.
  • Today’s recipe. Full quantities, full method, no seasoning cube anywhere in this book.
  • Today’s move. Mostly four minutes against a wall. Read Day 2 before you dismiss it.
  • Today’s secret. One thing a day you probably haven’t been told, that really does move the number.
  • This morning’s reading. A box to write it in. Fill it in every day.

Before you start, three rules

Do not stop any tablet. Nothing in these ten days replaces your medicine, and stopping it is the one thing here that can really hurt you.

Measure at the same time every morning, before food, before coffee, sitting, after five quiet minutes. If you take it any other way, you can’t compare the readings and they will only confuse you.

If any reading is 180/120 or above, stop the plan and go to hospital that day. Don’t try to manage that number at home.

All I ask is that you write down two numbers, ten days apart, and see for yourself.

Today’s recipe: Catfish Pepper Soup, no cube
Ingredients

Today’s secret: Check both arms, just this once

Take the reading on your left arm, then on your right. Write both down. From tomorrow, use whichever arm read higher, every time.

If the two arms differ by more than about 15 points on the top number, tell your doctor. A real gap between arms can mean a narrowed vessel, and it is something they will want to look at.

And check the cuff. A cuff that is too small for your arm reads falsely high, sometimes by 10 to 20 points. If you have a big arm, buy the large cuff. People have been treated for years on a number that was never real.

Ingredients

The sugar is the problem. Zobo made the usual way can be as sweet as a soft drink, and sugar works against you. Drink it unsweetened and cold, with ginger.

Two warnings. Zobo works alongside your tablet, so keep taking the tablet. And because zobo really does lower pressure, tell your doctor you are drinking it daily, especially if you take several medicines.

The best sources here are ordinary: ugu, waterleaf, beans, plantain, sweet potato, pawpaw, orange, coconut water, avocado (ube), tomato. None of it is imported or expensive.

One important exception. If you have been told you have kidney disease, or you take a tablet that holds potassium in, ask your doctor before you push potassium up. For that small group, too much potassium can be dangerous.

Why four minutes against a wall is worth doing

In 2023, researchers pooled 270 trials and 15,827 people to compare every kind of exercise for lowering blood pressure. Isometric work, where you hold a position without moving, came out on top at 8.2 points off the top number, compared with 4.5 for ordinary aerobic exercise and 4.1 for high-intensity training. The wall squat did best of all the forms they tested. It is free, it takes four minutes, and it needs nothing but a wall.

Chapter six

06

Your tablet, explained plainly

This is the most important chapter in the book, and it’s the one people skip.

What the tablet does

Different BP tablets work in different ways. Some relax the vessels, some take extra water and salt out through the kidney, and some slow the heart a little. What they all do is reduce the force pushing against your vessel walls, every hour of every day, including the hours you are asleep and the hours you are angry.

What they can’t do is cure it. Your pressure is low because the tablet is in your body.

Never stop it on your own

If you remember one thing from this book, remember this. People stop for ordinary, understandable reasons: the reading looked good, the money ran out, somebody at church said they had been healed, a mixture was recommended.

When your pressure goes back up after stopping, that only shows the tablet was working. With some BP medicines, stopping suddenly sends the pressure up higher than it was before you started, and that is when strokes happen.

If any of those reasons is about to apply to you, tell your doctor first and let them plan it with you. There are cheaper tablets and there are safe ways to change, but you have to go back and ask.

“BP drugs spoil kidney”

Let’s deal with this properly, because this one sentence has cost a lot of Nigerian lives.

It comes from something real. When you start certain BP tablets, a good doctor checks your kidney function a few weeks later, and sometimes the number shifts slightly. People hear “kidney test” and conclude the drug is attacking the kidney.

It is the other way round. The doctor does the test to protect your kidney. What is really destroying kidneys in this country is uncontrolled blood pressure. It is the second biggest cause of kidney failure in Nigeria, and dialysis costs more in a year than most families earn.

Your tablet is what stands between your kidney and the pressure.

Why it is usually for life

In most people, high blood pressure doesn’t come and go like malaria. It is how their body now controls its pressure, and when the tablet is taken away, the pressure goes back to where it was.

That sounds heavy until you compare it with a stroke at fifty-four. A tablet a day is the cheapest protection you can get.

A small number of people do come off medication with a doctor watching them. They are usually people whose pressure was only a little high, and who lost a lot of weight or cut out a great deal of salt. If that is going to be you, your doctor will tell you. Stopping on your own and hoping is not the way to find out.

When money is short

Say it out loud in the consulting room. Your doctor can’t help with a problem they don’t know about, and plenty of patients are in the same position.

  • Ask for the generic. The same drug without the brand name is often a fraction of the price and works just as well.
  • Ask for a combination tablet. Two drugs in one pill is frequently cheaper than two pills, and easier to remember.
  • Ask which one matters most if you really can’t afford all of them this month. Get the answer written down.
  • Buy a month at a time if you can. Buying five tablets at a time costs more per tablet, and that is how people end up with gaps.

Side effects: what to report, what passes

Report: a dry cough that will not stop, swelling of the ankles, feeling faint on standing, a rash. All of these have straightforward fixes, usually a change of tablet. None of them is a reason to stop on your own.

Usually passes: passing more urine in the first week, mild tiredness in the first few days. Give it two weeks before you judge.

Chapter seven

07

Measuring it yourself

One of the most useful things you can buy for your health this year is a blood pressure machine for the house.

Get a digital machine with a cuff that goes round your upper arm. Avoid the wrist ones, because they are much less reliable and the position of your hand changes the answer. And make sure the cuff fits you.

The cuff is the part nobody checks

A cuff that is too small for your arm reads falsely high, sometimes by 10 to 20 points. People with big arms have been treated for years on numbers that were never real, and others have been frightened into herbal mixtures by a reading that was wrong.

If the cuff does not wrap comfortably with room to spare, buy the large size. Say it in the shop: “I need the large cuff.”

The seven rules for a reading you can trust

  • Empty your bladder first. A full bladder adds about 10 points. This alone explains a lot of frightening morning readings.
  • Sit quietly for a full five minutes before you start. Thirty seconds is not enough.
  • Back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs uncrossed. Crossed legs add points.
  • Arm resting on a table at the level of your heart. An arm hanging down reads high; an arm raised reads low.
  • Put the cuff on bare skin, with no sleeve underneath.
  • Don’t talk, and don’t check your phone. Talking during a reading adds up to 10 points.
  • Take two readings a minute apart and write down the second one. The first is almost always the highest.

White coat

Plenty of people read high in a clinic and normal at home. It is common enough to have a name, and it is a good reason to bring your own log to appointments. A week of careful home readings tells your doctor far more than one number taken while you were anxious in a corridor.

The numbers that mean go now

180 over 120, or above

Repeat it once after five minutes of sitting. If it is still there, go to hospital the same day. Don’t wait till tomorrow, or until after church.

Go immediately, whatever the reading says, if any of these appear suddenly: chest pain, severe breathlessness, one side of the face dropping, weakness in one arm, speech that will not come, the worst headache of your life, or sudden loss of vision.

What a good week looks like on paper

Take two readings a week on the same mornings, and write both numbers down with the date. After a month you will have eight readings, which is enough for your doctor to see a pattern and act on it.

One high reading on its own doesn’t mean much. Don’t panic, and don’t change anything because of it. Write it down and keep going.

When you walk in with four weeks of your own readings, your doctor has something real to work with.

Chapter eight

08

Your household

High blood pressure runs in families, and you are the one who found out first, so you are in a good position to help the others.

Part of it is inherited, and part of it is that a family shares one kitchen, one salt habit, one way of cooking, and the same idea of what food should taste like. Change the kitchen and you change things for everybody in it.

Who should know their number

  • Your husband or wife. Check them today. You have a machine now, and it takes two minutes.
  • Your brothers and sisters. If it is in you, it is likely in them.
  • Your parents, if they are living. Many older Nigerians have never had a reading taken outside a hospital admission.
  • Everybody in the house over thirty. A reading once a year is enough.

The strongest reason to cook one way

A child raised in a low-salt kitchen grows up with a tongue that doesn’t need much salt, and a much lower chance of ever sitting where you are sitting. This is how it stops with you.

The conversation with a man who will not go

Almost every Nigerian family has one. He feels fine, he has never been sick a day, hospital is for weak people, and he is not going.

Arguing won’t work, but these three things sometimes do:

  • Instead of asking him to go to hospital, ask him to sit down for two minutes. You have the machine, so take it to him. It’s harder to wave away a number than a lecture.
  • Talk about the people who depend on him. A man who thinks nothing can touch him will often listen when it is about his children.
  • Show him the cube page. Men who will not discuss their health will happily argue about food, and that argument gets the salt out of the pot either way.
