# How these books should sound

Written after the owner read the 10X Fat-Burning Switch (then called the 10X Fat Switch) and said it sounded like AI.
He was right, and it was measurable: 115 em dashes in 11,286 words (10 per
thousand, where ordinary human prose runs at two to four), 23 "it is not X,
it is Y" sentences, and a lot of *genuinely*, *quietly* and *honestly*.

The voice to aim for is an older Nigerian doctor talking to a patient he
likes. Warm, direct, a little humour, full sentences that run on the way
people actually talk. Not a copywriter, and not a lecturer.

## What to stop doing

**Em dashes.** Two or three per thousand words at most. Most of them become
a full stop, a comma, "because", or brackets. Never two in one sentence.

**Contrast framing.** "It is not willpower, it is planning." "Not no amala.
Half." "This is not a diet. It is a way of eating." Just say the true thing
directly: "Planning is what gets you through the afternoon."

**Drama fragments.** "Same plate. Same food. Nothing removed." Three short
fragments in a row reads as an advert. Write one sentence.

**The tell words.** genuinely, quietly, honestly, precisely, exactly (as an
intensifier), truly, simply, the single most, the one thing, here is the
thing, that is the whole point, and that is why, which is precisely why.

**Tidy three-part lists** ending a paragraph for rhythm ("slow, unglamorous,
and permanent"). One real detail beats three adjectives.

**Aphorism endings.** A paragraph does not need to close on a quotable line.
Let most of them just end.

## What to do instead

**Name real things.** Malta Guinness, not "a malt". Agege bread. Pure water.
Mama put. The buka. Keke and danfo. Milo and Peak milk. Sunday rice. The
chemist. The provision shop. Owambe. Small chops. Puff-puff. Suya at night.

**Use Nigerian English the way people speak it.** "Dish your food." "Your
body will thank you." "Take it small small." "Before you know it."
"Chop" and light Pidgin belong in things people say, inside quote marks,
not in the narrator's own voice, and only a few times a book.

**Explain the way you would to your aunty.** If a sentence needs a biology
word, put the plain meaning next to it. Short words over long ones.

**Let sentences vary on their own.** Some long, some short, none of them
performing.

**Do not invent numbers to sound confident.** "Oil in stew has tripled in
thirty years" had no source and came out. If there is no figure, say "a lot
more than it used to be".
